Dilation of Aortic Valve, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a small tube, called a catheter, through an artery in the leg or arm to reach the aortic valve in the heart. A balloon-tipped device is then guided through the catheter to the valve, where it is inflated to widen the valve opening, improving blood flow from the heart to the rest of the body. The goal of this procedure is to reduce symptoms of aortic stenosis, such as shortness of breath, chest pain, and fainting, by increasing the flow of oxygen-rich blood to the brain and other organs. By using a percutaneous approach, this procedure avoids the need for open-heart surgery, reducing recovery time and risk of complications.

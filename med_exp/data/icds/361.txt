Dilation of Coronary Artery, Two Arteries with Two Drug-eluting Intraluminal Devices, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the use of a percutaneous approach to dilate two coronary arteries, which are narrowed or blocked due to plaque buildup, in order to restore blood flow to the heart. During the procedure, two drug-eluting intraluminal devices are inserted through the skin and guided through the arteries to the blockages, where they are expanded to widen the artery and release medication to prevent further narrowing. The devices are designed to slowly release the medication over time, helping to maintain the artery's patency and reduce the risk of future blockages. The goal of the procedure is to improve blood flow to the heart, relieve symptoms such as chest pain, and reduce the risk of heart attack.

Excision of Common Hepatic Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of a portion of the common hepatic duct, which is a vital structure that carries bile from the liver and pancreas to the small intestine. The procedure is typically performed through the mouth and digestive tract, using an endoscope equipped with specialized instruments to access the duct. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and potentially treat conditions such as bile duct stones, strictures, or tumors that may be obstructing the flow of bile. The procedure is usually performed under sedation or anesthesia to ensure patient comfort and safety.

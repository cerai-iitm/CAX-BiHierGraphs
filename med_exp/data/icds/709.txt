Dilation of Left Internal Carotid Artery with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves making a small incision in the groin area to access the femoral artery, through which a catheter is guided to the left internal carotid artery under fluoroscopic guidance. Once in place, a drug-eluting intraluminal device is deployed to dilate the narrowed or blocked segment of the artery, allowing for improved blood flow to the brain. The drug-eluting feature of the device helps to prevent restenosis, or the recurrence of blockage, by slowly releasing medication to the surrounding tissue. As a result, the risk of stroke and other complications associated with carotid artery disease are significantly reduced.

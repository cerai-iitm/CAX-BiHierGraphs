Extirpation of Matter from Cecum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During an endoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is inserted through the mouth or anus to reach the cecum, a pouch-like structure of the large intestine. The scope is guided to the area where the unwanted matter has accumulated, and specialized instruments are used to carefully remove the foreign substance. This minimally invasive approach allows for a precise and targeted removal of the material, minimizing damage to surrounding tissue and promoting a faster recovery. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation, and patients are often able to return to their normal activities within a few days.

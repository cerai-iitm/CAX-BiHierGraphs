Irrigation of Upper GI using Irrigating Substance, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
Upper gastrointestinal irrigation is a minimally invasive procedure used to cleanse the upper GI tract of debris, blood, or other substances. Through the use of an endoscope, a specialized instrument is inserted through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth or a gastric tube, to access the upper GI tract. A irrigating substance is then administered through the endoscope to flush out the targeted area, helping to restore normal digestive function and alleviate symptoms. This procedure is often performed in conjunction with other diagnostic or therapeutic procedures to provide a more comprehensive approach to patient care.

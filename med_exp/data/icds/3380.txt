Drainage of Pancreas, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
During an endoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is inserted through the mouth or a surgically created opening to access the pancreas, allowing a healthcare professional to visually examine the pancreas and identify any blockages or abnormalities. Once the area is visualized, a drainage device is inserted through the endoscope to remove any accumulated fluid, such as pus or pancreatic juice, that may be causing inflammation or infection. This minimally invasive procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation or local anesthesia to minimize discomfort and promote a swift recovery. By draining the pancreas, the procedure aims to alleviate symptoms such as abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting, and promote healing of the affected area.

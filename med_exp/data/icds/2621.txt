Bypass Ileum to Cutaneous, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves creating a bypass between the ileum, a part of the small intestine, and the skin, allowing digested food to pass directly from the small intestine to an artificial opening on the abdomen, thereby bypassing the entire large intestine. The open approach requires a large incision in the abdomen, allowing the surgeon to visually examine the intestine and perform the bypass. The artificial opening, known as a stoma, is created on the skin, and the ileum is attached to it, allowing waste to exit the body through the stoma instead of the anus. This procedure is usually performed to treat conditions such as cancer, Crohn's disease, or ulcerative colitis that require the removal of the large intestine.

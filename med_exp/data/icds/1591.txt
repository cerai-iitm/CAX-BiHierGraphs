Extirpation of Matter from Superior Mesenteric Vein, Percutaneous Approach
A minimally invasive procedure is performed to remove unwanted matter from the superior mesenteric vein, a vital blood vessel that supplies oxygenated blood to the small intestine. Through a small incision in the skin, a catheter or other specialized instrument is guided through the vein to reach the affected area. The offending material, which may be a blood clot, tumor, or other obstruction, is then carefully extracted or dissolved to restore normal blood flow. This percutaneous approach reduces recovery time and scarring compared to open surgery, allowing patients to rapidly resume normal activities.

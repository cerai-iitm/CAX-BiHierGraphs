Extirpation of Matter from Respiratory Tract, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This procedure involves using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, to visualize the respiratory tract and remove abnormal or unwanted matter, such as mucus, blood, or foreign objects, through a natural opening, like the mouth or nose, or an artificial opening, such as a tracheostomy tube. The endoscope is inserted through the opening and guided to the affected area, where specialized instruments are used to extract the unwanted material. This minimally invasive procedure allows for precise removal of the matter while minimizing damage to surrounding tissue and promoting healing. By extirpating the unwanted matter, this procedure can help restore normal respiratory function and alleviate symptoms such as coughing, wheezing, or shortness of breath.

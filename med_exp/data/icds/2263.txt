Extirpation of Matter from Right Middle Lobe Bronchus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During a minimally invasive endoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light is inserted through the mouth or nose to visualize the respiratory tract. The right middle lobe bronchus is then identified, and any abnormal material, such as mucus, blood, or other debris, is carefully removed using specialized instruments. This extirpation procedure aids in restoring normal airflow and pulmonary function. The procedure is typically performed under sedation or general anesthesia to ensure patient comfort throughout the process.

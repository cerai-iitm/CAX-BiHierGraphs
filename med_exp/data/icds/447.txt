Insertion of Pacemaker Lead into Coronary Vein, Percutaneous Approach
During a minimally invasive procedure, a pacemaker lead is inserted through the skin and into a coronary vein, which is a blood vessel that supplies oxygenated blood to the heart muscle. The lead is carefully guided through the vein using fluoroscopy, a real-time x-ray imaging technology, to ensure accurate placement. Once in position, the pacemaker lead is secured in place and connected to a pacemaker device, which regulates the heart's rhythm to treat conditions such as bradycardia or heart block. The percutaneous approach allows for smaller incisions and reduced risk of complications compared to traditional open-chest surgery.

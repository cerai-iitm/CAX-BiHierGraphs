Reposition Left Upper Femur with Internal Fixation Device, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves repositioning the upper end of the left femur (thigh bone) into its normal anatomic alignment using a minimally invasive percutaneous approach. The surgeon makes small incisions in the skin and uses specialized instruments to reposition the bone, and then secures it in place with an internal fixation device. The internal fixation device, such as a rod or plate, provides stability to the bone as it heals, and can be left in place permanently or removed after the bone has fully recovered. This procedure is often performed to treat fractures, bone deformities, or other conditions that affect the alignment and stability of the upper femur.

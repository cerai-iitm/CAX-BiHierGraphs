Fusion of Lumbosacral Joint with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves fusing the lumbosacral joint, which connects the lowest part of the spine to the sacrum, using a nonautologous tissue substitute, such as bone graft or artificial material. The procedure is performed through an open approach, where the surgeon makes an incision in the posterior (back) of the body to access the affected joint. The posterior column, which includes the pedicles and vertebral bodies, is the target area for the fusion. The goal of this procedure is to stabilize the joint and alleviate symptoms such as pain, numbness, and tingling in the lower back and legs.

Insertion of Feeding Device into Stomach, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) is a minimally invasive procedure that involves the insertion of a feeding tube through the abdominal wall and into the stomach. During the procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is guided through the mouth and down the esophagus to visualize the stomach. The tube is then pushed through the abdominal wall and into the stomach, allowing for the delivery of nutrition directly into the stomach. This procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation and local anesthesia, and is often recommended for patients who have difficulty swallowing or require long-term nutritional support.

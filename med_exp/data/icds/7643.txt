Introduction of Other Thrombolytic into Peripheral Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the percutaneous introduction of a thrombolytic agent into a peripheral artery to dissolve blood clots and restore blood flow. Through a small incision in the skin, a catheter is inserted into the affected artery and guided to the site of the blockage. The thrombolytic medication is then infused through the catheter to break down the clot and restore patency to the vessel. This minimally invasive procedure is often used to treat peripheral artery occlusions, such as those caused by acute limb ischemia or deep vein thrombosis.

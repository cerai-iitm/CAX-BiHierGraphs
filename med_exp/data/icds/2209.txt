Excision of Right Middle Lung Lobe, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive surgical procedure involves the removal of the right middle lung lobe through a small incision in the chest wall using an endoscope, a thin, flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, which allows the surgeon to visualize the inside of the lung. The percutaneous endoscopic approach allows for reduced trauma to the surrounding tissue, resulting in less pain, bleeding, and scarring compared to traditional open surgery. During the procedure, the lung lobe is carefully dissected and separated from surrounding tissues and blood vessels, and then removed through the small incision. The surgeon will then close the incision, usually with sutures or staples, to complete the procedure.

Extirpation of Matter from Common Bile Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During this minimally invasive procedure, an endoscope is inserted through the mouth or a surgical incision to access the common bile duct. The scope is equipped with specialized instruments that allow the physician to remove stones, sludge, or other obstructions from the duct, restoring proper bile flow and alleviating symptoms such as jaundice, abdominal pain, and nausea. The endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications and allows for a faster recovery compared to traditional open surgery. Once the blockage is removed, the duct is flushed to ensure patency and the scope is withdrawn, leaving the patient with improved digestion and liver function.

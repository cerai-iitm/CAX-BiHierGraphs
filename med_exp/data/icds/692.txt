Dilation of Intracranial Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a thin, flexible tube called a catheter through a small incision in the groin, which is then guided through the blood vessels to the intracranial artery. Once in place, a tiny balloon is inflated to widen the narrowed or blocked artery, restoring blood flow to the brain. The catheter is then removed, and the incision is closed. This percutaneous approach allows for quicker recovery and fewer complications compared to open surgery.

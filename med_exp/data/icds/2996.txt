Insertion of Feeding Device into Jejunum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening
This procedure involves the surgical placement of a feeding tube into the jejunum, a part of the small intestine, through a natural or artificial opening in the abdominal wall. The tube is designed to provide nutrition directly into the jejunum, bypassing the stomach, in patients who have difficulty eating or absorbing food orally. The insertion is typically performed under general anesthesia or conscious sedation, and requires a small incision in the abdomen or use of existing openings, such as a stoma. Post-procedure, the patient is usually monitored for a few hours to ensure proper tube function and to address any potential complications.

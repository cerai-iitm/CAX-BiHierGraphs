Introduction of Destructive Agent into Upper GI, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a specialized instrument through the mouth or a surgical opening to access the upper gastrointestinal (GI) tract, which includes the esophagus, stomach, and duodenum. Once in place, a destructive agent, such as a sclerosant or a cytoablative substance, is introduced through the instrument to treat conditions like bleeding varices, active bleeding, or tumors. The agent works by causing targeted damage to the affected area, promoting hemostasis or reducing tumor size. The entire process is visually guided by real-time imaging and monitoring to ensure accurate placement and minimize potential complications.

Excision of Common Bile Duct, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This percutaneous procedure involves the removal of a sample of the common bile duct tissue for diagnostic purposes, utilizing imaging guidance to accurately target the area of interest. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and sedation to minimize discomfort, and involves the insertion of a small needle or catheter through the skin and into the bile duct. The obtained tissue sample is then sent to a laboratory for examination, aiding in the diagnosis of conditions affecting the bile duct, such as malignancies, strictures, or stones. The entire process is usually completed on an outpatient basis, allowing the patient to recover quickly and resume normal activities shortly after the procedure.

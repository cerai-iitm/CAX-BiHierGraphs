Excision of Chest Skin, External Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the surgical removal of skin tissue from the chest area through an external approach, typically performed under general anesthesia or local anesthesia with sedation. The excision is usually done to diagnose skin lesions, cancers, or other abnormalities that require a tissue sample for further examination. The surgery involves making an incision in the skin, dissecting the affected area, and removing the skin tissue, which is then sent to a laboratory for histopathological examination. The excision site is then closed with sutures or staples, and the patient is monitored for any post-operative complications.

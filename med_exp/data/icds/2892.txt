Excision of Sigmoid Colon, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the removal of a portion of the sigmoid colon through a natural or artificial opening using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end. The endoscope is inserted through the natural opening of the anus or an artificial opening created in the abdominal wall, allowing the surgeon to visualize the colon and perform the excision. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may be necessary to treat conditions such as colon cancer, diverticulitis, or bowel obstruction. The endoscopic approach offers several benefits, including less postoperative pain, reduced scarring, and a faster recovery time compared to traditional open surgery.

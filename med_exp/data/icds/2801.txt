Excision of Lower Esophagus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, to visualize the lower esophagus through a natural or artificial opening. The surgeon then uses specialized instruments to carefully excise the targeted area of the esophagus, allowing for precise removal of abnormal tissue. The endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications and promotes faster recovery compared to open surgery. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may be used to treat conditions such as esophageal cancer, Barrett's esophagus, or high-grade dysplasia.

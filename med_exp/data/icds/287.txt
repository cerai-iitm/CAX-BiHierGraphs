Bypass Coronary Artery, One Artery from Aorta with Autologous Arterial Tissue, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves creating a detour around a blocked or narrowed section of one coronary artery, restoring blood flow to the heart muscle. The surgeon uses a piece of the patient's own arterial tissue taken from another part of the body to create a bypass graft, connecting it to the aorta and the blocked coronary artery. The procedure is performed through an open approach, which means the surgeon makes a large incision in the chest to access the heart and its arteries. By bypassing the blockage, the graft allows oxygen-rich blood to flow freely to the heart muscle, reducing symptoms such as chest pain and shortness of breath.

Fusion of 2 or more Cervical Vertebral Joints with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Anterior Column, Open Approach
During this surgical procedure, the vertebral joints in the cervical spine are fused together using an autologous tissue substitute, which is a graft taken from the patient's own body, to stabilize the spine and alleviate pain. The procedure involves making an incision in the back of the neck to access the spine, and the graft is then inserted through an open approach, which means the surgical site is open and visible to the surgeon. The anterior column, or front part of the spine, is the focus of the fusion, and the autologous tissue substitute is used to facilitate bone growth and fusion between the adjacent vertebrae. The goal of the procedure is to restore spinal stability, reduce pain, and improve mobility in the neck and surrounding areas.

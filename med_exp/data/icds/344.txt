Dilation of Coronary Artery, One Artery, Bifurcation, with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves accessing the coronary artery through the skin, using a percutaneous approach, to treat blockages at a bifurcation point, where the artery divides into two branches. A small, flexible tube called a catheter is guided through the artery to the blocked area, and a drug-eluting intraluminal device, such as a stent, is deployed to widen the artery and restore blood flow. The stent is coated with medication that helps prevent the artery from narrowing again, and it is designed to dissolve over time, leaving behind a healthy, functioning artery. The goal of this procedure is to relieve symptoms such as chest pain, reduce the risk of heart attack, and improve overall heart health.

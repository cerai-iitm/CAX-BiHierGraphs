Excision of Cecum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
During a minimally invasive endoscopic procedure, the cecum, a part of the large intestine, is surgically removed through a natural or artificial opening. This procedure allows for a precise and targeted approach, reducing trauma to surrounding tissues and promoting faster recovery. The use of an endoscope provides visualization of the internal structures, enabling the surgeon to accurately identify and excise the cecum. This diagnostic procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as colon cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, or bleeding in the cecum.

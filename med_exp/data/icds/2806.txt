Excision of Esophagogastric Junction, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
Endoscopic excision of the esophagogastric junction is a minimally invasive procedure used to remove abnormal tissue or lesions at the junction where the esophagus meets the stomach. During the procedure, an endoscope is inserted through the mouth or a small incision to visualize the affected area and specialized instruments are used to excise the targeted tissue. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may take several hours to complete. Post-procedure, patients may experience some discomfort and may need to follow a liquid diet for a few days to aid in the healing process.

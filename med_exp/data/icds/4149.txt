Insertion of Pacemaker, Dual Chamber into Chest Subcutaneous Tissue and Fascia, Open Approach
Here is a detailed description of the procedure:

A dual-chamber pacemaker is surgically inserted into the chest tissue and fascia through an open approach, providing regulated heartbeat rhythms to patients with abnormal heart rhythms or rates. The pacemaker is placed under the skin, usually just below the collarbone, and connected to the heart through insulated wires called leads. The open approach allows the surgeon to visually access the implant site, ensuring proper placement and securing the device to surrounding tissue. This procedure is often performed under local anesthesia and sedation, with the entire process typically taking around 1-2 hours to complete.

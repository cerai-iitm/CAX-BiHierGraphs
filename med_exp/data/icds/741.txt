Excision of Right Temporal Artery, Open Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves making an incision in the skin to access the right temporal artery, which is a major blood vessel located in the temple region. The artery is then surgically dissected and a section of it is removed for diagnostic purposes, such as to test for vascular diseases or to identify the presence of abnormal cells. The excision is typically performed under general anesthesia, and the incision site is closed with sutures or staples after the procedure. The removed section of the artery is then sent to a laboratory for further examination and testing.

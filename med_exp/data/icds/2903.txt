Excision of Anus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the excision of the anus through a natural or artificial opening for diagnostic purposes, usually performed under anesthesia to minimize discomfort. The surgical removal of the anus is typically done to examine the tissue and identify any abnormalities, such as cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, or precancerous cells. During the procedure, the surgeon makes an incision in the natural or artificial opening to access the anus, carefully removes the affected tissue, and sends it for further examination. The goal of this diagnostic excision is to provide a precise diagnosis and develop an effective treatment plan.

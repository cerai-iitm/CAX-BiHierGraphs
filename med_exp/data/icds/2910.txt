Excision of Greater Omentum, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves making an incision in the abdominal wall to access the greater omentum, a fatty apron-like structure that hangs from the stomach and contains blood vessels and lymphatic vessels. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia, and the surgeon carefully dissects the greater omentum from its attachments to surrounding organs and tissues. The entire greater omentum or a portion of it is then removed, and the incision is closed, usually with sutures or staples. This procedure is often performed to treat omental tumors, inflammation, or injuries, or to improve access to other organs during abdominal surgery.

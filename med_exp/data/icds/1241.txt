Occlusion of Inferior Mesenteric Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of an intraluminal device through the skin to occlude the inferior mesenteric artery, a blood vessel that supplies oxygenated blood to the large intestine. The procedure is typically performed under imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, to ensure precise placement of the device. The occlusion of the inferior mesenteric artery is often used to treat conditions such as bleeding or aneurysms in the artery. By blocking blood flow to the artery, the device helps to prevent further bleeding or rupture, promoting healing and reducing the risk of complications.

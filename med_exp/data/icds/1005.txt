Dilation of Superior Mesenteric Artery with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Open Approach
This procedure involves accessing the superior mesenteric artery through an open incision, allowing the surgeon to directly visualize the blood vessel. A drug-eluting intraluminal device, such as a stent, is then placed within the artery to dilate the vessel and improve blood flow. The device is coated with medication that helps prevent the growth of new blockages and reduces the risk of restenosis. By opening up the narrowed or blocked artery, this procedure can help to relieve symptoms such as abdominal pain, nausea, and weight loss.

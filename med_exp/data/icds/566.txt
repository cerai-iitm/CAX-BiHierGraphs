Replacement of Mitral Valve with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves replacing the diseased or damaged mitral valve with a non-autologous tissue substitute, such as a bioprosthetic valve, through an open approach. During the procedure, the surgeon makes an incision in the chest to access the heart, and the diseased valve is removed and replaced with the new tissue substitute. The prosthetic valve is designed to mimic the function of the natural mitral valve, allowing for proper blood flow between the heart's chambers. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may require a stay in the hospital for several days to recover.

Destruction of Gallbladder, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves making a large incision in the abdomen to access the gallbladder, which is then removed from the body. The open approach allows for direct visualization and manual dissection of the gallbladder and its surrounding tissues, enabling the surgeon to carefully separate it from the liver and other adjacent structures. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may take several hours to complete, depending on the complexity of the case. After the gallbladder is removed, the incision is closed, usually with sutures or staples, and the patient is taken to the recovery room for post-operative care.

Excision of Thymus, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive surgical procedure involves the removal of the thymus gland through small incisions using an endoscope, a thin, flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, which is inserted through the skin to visualize the area. The thymus gland, located in the chest, is carefully dissected and excised using specialized instruments, minimizing tissue damage and promoting a quick recovery. This approach reduces the risk of complications and scarring, while allowing for precise visualization and removal of the targeted tissue. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia, and the patient is usually able to return to normal activities soon after the operation.

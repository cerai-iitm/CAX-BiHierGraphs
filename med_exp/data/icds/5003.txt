Insertion of Internal Fixation Device into Cervical Vertebra, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the insertion of an internal fixation device into the cervical vertebra through an open approach, which means a surgical incision is made to access the spine. The internal fixation device is used to stabilize and align the vertebrae, providing support and promoting healing. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia, and the surgeon uses imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or X-ray, to ensure accurate placement of the device. The goal of the procedure is to restore spinal stability, relieve pressure on surrounding nerves and muscles, and alleviate symptoms such as pain, numbness, or weakness in the neck and arms.

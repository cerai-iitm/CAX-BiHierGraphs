Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Heart, Percutaneous Approach
This percutaneous procedure involves the introduction of a therapeutic substance directly into the heart through a small incision in the skin. The substance is injected through a catheter inserted into a blood vessel, usually in the arm or leg, and guided to the heart using fluoroscopy or other imaging techniques. The goal of the procedure is to deliver the therapeutic agent directly to the heart tissue to treat conditions such as cardiac arrhythmias, myocardial infarction, or heart failure. The percutaneous approach minimizes the risk of complications and allows for a faster recovery compared to open-chest surgery.

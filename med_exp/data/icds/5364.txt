Removal of External Fixation Device from Right Tibia, Percutaneous Approach
The removal of an external fixation device from the right tibia is a percutaneous procedure that involves the surgical extraction of a temporary stabilizing device from the outer surface of the tibia bone in the lower leg. This device is typically used to hold the bone in place while it heals from a fracture or osteotomy, and its removal marks the final stage of the healing process. During the procedure, the surgeon makes small incisions in the skin to access the device, carefully disassembles it, and removes it from the bone, taking care to minimize tissue damage and promote optimal healing. The entire process is usually performed under local anesthesia or conscious sedation to ensure patient comfort.

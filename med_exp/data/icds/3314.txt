Dilation of Ampulla of Vater with Intraluminal Device, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During an endoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light is guided through the mouth and stomach into the small intestine to reach the ampulla of Vater, a muscular valve-like structure that regulates the flow of bile and pancreatic juices. A specialized intraluminal device is then advanced through the endoscope and positioned within the ampulla to gently dilate, or widen, the valve to improve the flow of bile and pancreatic juices. This minimally invasive procedure is performed to relieve obstruction or narrowing of the ampulla, which can cause symptoms such as abdominal pain, jaundice, and pancreatic enzyme elevations. The goal of the dilation is to restore normal digestive function and alleviate symptoms.

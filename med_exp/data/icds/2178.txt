Excision of Right Upper Lobe Bronchus, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive diagnostic procedure involves the removal of tissue from the right upper lobe bronchus, a section of the airway that brings air into the right lung, through a small incision in the skin. The percutaneous approach allows for a quicker recovery time and less post-operative pain compared to open surgery. During the procedure, a small instrument is guided through the incision to the affected area, allowing the healthcare provider to retrieve a sample of tissue for examination. This diagnostic excision helps to identify any abnormalities or diseases in the bronchus, such as cancer or inflammation.

Excision of Left Atrial Appendage, Open Approach
During cardiac surgery, the left atrial appendage is surgically removed through an open approach to reduce the risk of stroke and blood clot formation. This procedure involves making an incision in the chest to access the heart, and carefully excising the left atrial appendage, a small pouch-like structure in the upper chamber of the heart. The removal of the left atrial appendage can be performed as a standalone procedure or in conjunction with other cardiac surgeries, such as heart valve repair or coronary artery bypass grafting. The goal of this procedure is to minimize the risk of stroke and prevent blood clots from forming in the left atrial appendage, which can occur in individuals with atrial fibrillation or other heart conditions.

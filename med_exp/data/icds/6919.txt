Drainage of Retroperitoneum, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the insertion of a drainage tube or catheter through the skin and into the retroperitoneal space, a region behind the peritoneum that contains the kidneys, adrenal glands, and other structures. The goal of this percutaneous procedure is to drain excess fluid, blood, or pus that has accumulated in the retroperitoneum, which can be caused by various conditions such as injury, infection, or surgery. Guided by imaging studies such as CT or ultrasound, the healthcare provider uses a needle or small incision to access the retroperitoneum and drain the accumulated fluid. The diagnostic aspect of this procedure helps identify the underlying cause of the fluid accumulation and guides further treatment.

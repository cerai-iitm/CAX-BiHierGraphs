Plain Radiography of Multiple Coronary Arteries using Low Osmolar Contrast
This imaging procedure involves the use of low osmolar contrast material to visualize multiple coronary arteries. The process begins with the insertion of a catheter into an artery, typically in the groin or arm, through which the contrast agent is injected. Once the contrast material is in place, plain radiography, also known as X-ray, is used to capture images of the coronary arteries, providing detailed information on blood flow and vessel structure. The resulting images help diagnose and monitor coronary artery disease, blockages, and other vascular conditions.

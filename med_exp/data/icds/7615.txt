Introduction of Platelet Inhibitor into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the percutaneous introduction of a platelet inhibitor into a peripheral vein, typically through a needle or catheter inserted into the vein. The platelet inhibitor, such as clopidogrel or prasugrel, is administered to prevent platelet activation and aggregation, which can lead to thrombus formation and cardiovascular events. The percutaneous approach allows for a minimally invasive and relatively painless procedure, reducing the risk of complications and promoting rapid recovery. The aim of this treatment is to reduce the risk of cardiovascular events, such as heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease, in patients with acute coronary syndrome or other cardiovascular conditions.

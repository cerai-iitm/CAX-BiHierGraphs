Fluoroscopy of Left Subclavian Artery using Other Contrast
During this diagnostic procedure, a contrast agent other than iodine or barium is injected into the left subclavian artery to enhance image quality. Real-time X-ray fluoroscopy is then used to guide the procedure and capture detailed images of the artery's structure and blood flow. The resulting images help diagnose blockages, narrowing, or other abnormalities in the left subclavian artery, which supplies blood to the arm and hand. The procedure is typically performed by an interventional radiologist or cardiologist in a hospital or outpatient setting.

Fusion of Thoracic Vertebral Joint with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves combining two or more vertebrae in the thoracic region of the spine using a non-autologous tissue substitute, such as bone graft or synthetic material, to promote fusion and stabilize the joint. The posterior approach is used, where the surgeon accesses the spine through an incision in the back, to reach the posterior column of the vertebrae. An open approach is used, which involves a larger incision to allow for direct visualization of the spine. The goal of this procedure is to alleviate pain and improve mobility by fusing the vertebrae together, which can help to correct conditions such as scoliosis, spondylolisthesis, or degenerative disc disease.

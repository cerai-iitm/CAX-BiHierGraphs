Reposition Thoracic Vertebra with Internal Fixation Device, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the correction of a thoracic vertebral misalignment or deformity by repositioning the affected vertebrae using an open approach, which requires a surgical incision to access the spine. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and involves the insertion of an internal fixation device, such as rods, screws, or plates, to stabilize the spine and maintain the corrected position. The internal fixation device helps to promote bone fusion and healing, allowing the spine to return to its natural alignment and alleviating symptoms such as back pain, numbness, or tingling. The goal of the procedure is to restore spinal stability, improve mobility, and enhance the patient's overall quality of life.

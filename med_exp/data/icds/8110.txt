Fluoroscopy of Aorta and Bilateral Lower Extremity Arteries using Other Contrast
This non-invasive imaging procedure involves the use of a contrast agent to visualize the aorta and bilateral lower extremity arteries, allowing healthcare professionals to diagnose and monitor conditions affecting blood flow to the legs, such as peripheral artery disease or atherosclerosis. During the procedure, a specialized dye is injected into a vein, and then fluoroscopy, a type of X-ray, is used to track the flow of the contrast agent through the aorta and arteries in the legs. This helps to identify any blockages, narrowing, or other abnormalities in the blood vessels. The procedure is typically performed in a hospital or outpatient imaging center by a trained radiologist or technologist.

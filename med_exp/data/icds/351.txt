Dilation of Coronary Artery, One Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the use of a percutaneous approach to access the coronary artery, which is then dilated using an intraluminal device to widen the narrowed or blocked artery and restore blood flow. The device is inserted through a small incision in the skin and guided through the artery to the blockage, where it is expanded to compress the plaque against the artery wall. Once the artery is dilated, the device is removed, and the incision is closed. The goal of this procedure is to relieve symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain or shortness of breath, and reduce the risk of heart attack.

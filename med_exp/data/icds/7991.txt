Fluoroscopy of Thoracic Aorta using Low Osmolar Contrast
This diagnostic imaging procedure utilizes low osmolar contrast to enhance visualization of the thoracic aorta, a vital segment of the aorta that spans from the aortic arch to the diaphragm. During the procedure, a contrast agent is injected into a peripheral vein, and then fluoroscopy, a continuous X-ray beam, is used to capture real-time images of the thoracic aorta. This non-invasive examination helps healthcare providers diagnose and monitor various conditions affecting the thoracic aorta, such as aneurysms, dissections, and stenosis. The use of low osmolar contrast minimizes the risk of adverse reactions and allergic responses, ensuring a safer examination for patients.

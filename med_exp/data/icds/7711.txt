Introduction of Adhesion Barrier into Peritoneal Cavity, Open Approach
During abdominal surgery, a thin layer of biocompatible material is inserted into the peritoneal cavity to prevent the formation of adhesions, which are bands of fibrous tissue that can bind organs together. This synthetic or natural barrier reduces the risk of postoperative complications, such as bowel obstruction or chronic pain, by minimizing the attachment of tissues to the peritoneum. The open approach involves making a larger incision to visualize the surgical site and accurately position the adhesion barrier. By reducing adhesion formation, this procedure can improve patient outcomes and reduce the need for future surgical interventions.

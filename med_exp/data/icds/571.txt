Replacement of Tricuspid Valve with Zooplastic Tissue, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves introducing a catheter through a blood vessel in the leg or arm to reach the tricuspid valve, which is then replaced with a zooplastic tissue valve. The zooplastic tissue, typically derived from animal sources, is designed to mimic the function of a natural valve, allowing for proper blood flow between the heart's upper and lower chambers. The percutaneous approach eliminates the need for open-heart surgery, reducing recovery time and minimizing scarring. The replacement valve is carefully positioned and secured to ensure optimal cardiac function and alleviate symptoms such as fatigue, shortness of breath, and swelling.

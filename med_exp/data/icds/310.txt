Bypass Coronary Artery, Three Arteries from Aorta with Autologous Arterial Tissue, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves using a section of the patient's own arterial tissue to create a bypass around blocked or narrowed coronary arteries, restoring blood flow to the heart. The open approach requires a sternotomy, where the chest is opened to access the heart, and the procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia. The autologous arterial tissue is harvested from the patient's own body, usually from the radial or ulnar artery, and grafted onto the coronary arteries to create a detour around the blockages. By bypassing three coronary arteries, this procedure helps to alleviate symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain and shortness of breath.

Introduction of Other Gas into Lower GI, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive endoscopic procedure involves the introduction of a gas, such as carbon dioxide or air, into the lower gastrointestinal tract through a natural or artificial opening. The gas is inserted using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and specialized instruments, to inspect and treat conditions within the lower GI tract. This procedure may be performed to improve visualization during endoscopic procedures, reduce discomfort, or facilitate certain therapeutic interventions. The use of gas insufflation during endoscopy can also help to distend the digestive tract, allowing for more thorough examination and treatment of conditions such as colon polyps or GI bleeds.

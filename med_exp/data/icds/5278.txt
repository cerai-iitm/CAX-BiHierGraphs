Insertion of Intramedullary Internal Fixation Device into Right Upper Femur, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the insertion of an intramedullary internal fixation device into the right upper femur through a percutaneous approach, where a small incision is made in the skin to access the bone. The device is then inserted into the medullary canal of the femur to provide internal fixation and support to the fractured or damaged bone. This minimally invasive approach allows for reduced tissue damage and scarring compared to an open surgical approach. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or CT scans, to ensure accurate placement of the device.

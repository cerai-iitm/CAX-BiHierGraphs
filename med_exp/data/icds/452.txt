Insertion of Pacemaker Lead into Right Atrium, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a pacemaker lead into the right atrium of the heart through a percutaneous approach, which means the lead is inserted through the skin using a thin needle. The lead is guided through a vein, usually in the chest, and advanced to the right atrium, where it is attached to the heart muscle. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and fluoroscopic guidance, allowing the doctor to visualize the lead as it is inserted. The lead is then connected to a pacemaker, which regulates the heartbeat and ensures a normal heart rhythm.

Restriction of Thoracic Aorta, Descending with Branched or Fenestrated Intraluminal Device, Three or More Arteries, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the placement of a branched or fenestrated intraluminal device through a percutaneous approach to restrict blood flow in the descending thoracic aorta, where it diverges into three or more arteries. The device is designed to accommodate the complex anatomy of the aorta and its branches, allowing for precise control of blood flow. During the procedure, a catheter is guided through an artery in the leg or arm to the target site, where the device is deployed and expanded to fit the aortic anatomy. The goal of the procedure is to effectively manage conditions such as aortic aneurysms or dissections by reducing blood flow and promoting healing.

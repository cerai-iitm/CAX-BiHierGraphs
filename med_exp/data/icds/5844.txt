Drainage of Right Hip Joint, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
During a percutaneous procedure, a small incision is made in the skin to access the right hip joint, allowing for the insertion of a drainage device to remove excess fluid or debris that may be causing discomfort or impeding joint function. The procedure is typically performed under image guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, to ensure accurate placement of the drainage device. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and treat conditions such as septic arthritis, infection, or inflammation of the hip joint. After the procedure, the incision site is cleaned and dressed, and the patient is monitored for any signs of complications or infection.

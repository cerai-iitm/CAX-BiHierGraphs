Introduction of Monoclonal Antibody into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
During this minimally invasive procedure, a monoclonal antibody is introduced into a peripheral vein through a small puncture in the skin. The antibody is administered through a thin catheter or needle inserted into a vein in the arm or hand, allowing for precise delivery of the medication. This percutaneous approach allows for direct access to the bloodstream, enabling the antibody to target specific cells or proteins in the body. The procedure is typically performed in a clinical setting, such as a hospital or outpatient facility, under the supervision of a trained healthcare professional.

Extirpation of Matter from Left Lower Lung Lobe, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During a minimally invasive procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, known as an endoscope, is inserted through the mouth or nose to reach the left lower lung lobe. The endoscope allows the healthcare provider to visualize the lung tissue and identify the area of concern. The provider then uses specialized instruments to remove the unwanted matter, such as mucus or debris, from the lung lobe through the endoscope. This procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia or conscious sedation to ensure the patient's comfort and safety.

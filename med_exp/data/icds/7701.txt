Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Lower GI, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This procedure involves the insertion of a therapeutic substance through the natural or artificial opening of the lower gastrointestinal (GI) tract using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end. The substance is delivered directly into the lower GI, which includes the colon, rectum, and anus, to treat various conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, gastrointestinal infections, or cancer. The endoscope guides the insertion of the substance, allowing for precise placement and minimizing the risk of complications. The procedure is usually performed under conscious sedation or anesthesia to ensure patient comfort during the procedure.

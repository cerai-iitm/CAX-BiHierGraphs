Dilation of Lower Esophagus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end through the mouth or a previously created opening to reach the lower esophagus. Once in position, the tube is used to dilate or stretch the esophagus to its normal diameter, usually to treat conditions such as achalasia, diffuse esophageal spasm, or esophageal stricture. The dilation is typically performed using a balloon or bougie dilator, which is guided by the endoscope to ensure accurate placement. The goal of the procedure is to relieve symptoms such as difficulty swallowing, regurgitation, and chest pain by restoring the normal flow of food and liquids through the esophagus.

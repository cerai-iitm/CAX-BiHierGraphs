Drainage of Peritoneal Cavity, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the insertion of a drainage tube or catheter through the skin and into the peritoneal cavity, a fluid-filled space in the abdomen, to withdraw excess fluid or pus. This is typically done using imaging guidance, such as ultrasound or CT scan, to ensure accurate placement of the drainage tube. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and treat conditions such as peritonitis, an infection of the peritoneum, or to relieve symptoms of abdominal distension or pain. The drainage tube is left in place until the fluid or pus has been fully drained, and the patient is closely monitored for signs of infection or complications.

Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Pleural Cavity, Percutaneous Approach
This is a minimally invasive procedure in which a therapeutic substance is introduced into the pleural cavity, the space between the lungs and chest wall, through a small incision in the skin. The aim is to treat conditions such as pleural effusion, where there is an abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space, or to manage symptoms of malignant pleural effusion, where cancer has spread to the pleura. Under image guidance, a thin needle is inserted through the skin and into the pleural cavity, and the therapeutic substance is injected to help drain excess fluid, reduce inflammation, or prevent further fluid buildup. This percutaneous approach is less invasive than open surgical methods and can be performed under local anesthesia, reducing recovery time and minimizing scarring.

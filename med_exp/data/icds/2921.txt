Excision of Omentum, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the removal of the omentum, a fold of fatty tissue in the abdomen, through small incisions in the skin. A laparoscope, a thin tube with a camera and light, is inserted through one of the incisions to visualize the abdominal cavity. The omentum is then grasped with a surgical instrument and excised, allowing for the collection of tissue samples for diagnostic examination. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and is often used to diagnose and stage certain types of cancer, such as ovarian or colon cancer.

Reposition Right Tibia, External Approach
The repositioning procedure involves a surgical approach to realign the right tibia, which is the larger of the two bones in the lower leg, to its proper anatomical position. This is typically done to correct deformities or injuries that affect the bone's alignment, such as fractures or bowed legs. The external approach requires an incision in the skin and soft tissue to access the affected area, allowing the surgeon to manipulate the bone into its correct position. Once repositioned, the bone is stabilized using internal fixation devices, such as plates, screws, or rods, to maintain its new alignment during the healing process.

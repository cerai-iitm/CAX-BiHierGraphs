Release Omentum, Open Approach
During an open surgery, the omentum, a double layer of fatty tissue that attaches the stomach to other organs in the abdominal cavity, is carefully dissected and freed from adhesions or attachments to surrounding structures, allowing for increased mobility and restoration of normal anatomical relationships. This procedure is often performed to alleviate symptoms of abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting caused by omental adhesions or twisting. The open approach provides direct visualization of the omentum and surrounding tissues, enabling the surgeon to accurately identify and release the affected area. Following release, the omentum is typically repositioned to its normal anatomical location, allowing for proper digestive function and pain relief.

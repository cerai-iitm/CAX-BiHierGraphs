Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Peripheral Nerves and Plexi, Percutaneous Approach
This percutaneous procedure involves the insertion of a therapeutic substance into the peripheral nerves and plexi, typically through a small needle or cannula, to relieve pain, inflammation, or other conditions. The substance may be a steroid, anesthetic, or other medication, and is carefully targeted to the specific nerve or plexus responsible for the symptoms. The procedure is usually performed under imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, to ensure accurate placement of the substance. The goal of the procedure is to provide temporary or long-term relief from chronic pain, numbness, or tingling in the affected area.

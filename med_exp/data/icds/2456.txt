Drainage of Buccal Mucosa with Drainage Device, Open Approach
During an open surgical procedure, the buccal mucosa, a thin layer of tissue lining the inside of the cheek, is drained to remove excess fluid, blood, or pus that may have accumulated due to infection or injury. A drainage device is inserted through an incision made in the mucosa to facilitate the evacuation of the unwanted material. The device helps to create a pathway for the fluid to exit the affected area, promoting healing and reducing the risk of further complications. By performing this procedure, the surgeon can restore normal oral function and alleviate symptoms such as pain, swelling, and difficulty speaking or eating.

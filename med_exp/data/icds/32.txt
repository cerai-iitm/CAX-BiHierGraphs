Drainage of Cerebral Ventricle, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a small catheter or drainage tube through the skin and into the cerebral ventricle to remove excess cerebrospinal fluid. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and potentially treat conditions such as hydrocephalus, cerebral edema, or meningitis. Under image guidance, the physician carefully advances the catheter through the skull to access the ventricle, allowing for the collection of a sample of cerebrospinal fluid for further analysis. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and is often used to guide further treatment decisions, such as the placement of a shunt or antibiotics.

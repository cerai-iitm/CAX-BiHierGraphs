Bypass Coronary Artery, One Artery from Right Internal Mammary, Open Approach
A coronary artery bypass grafting procedure involves the use of the right internal mammary artery to bypass a blocked or narrowed coronary artery, restoring blood flow to the heart. During the open approach, the surgeon makes an incision in the chest to access the heart and graft the internal mammary artery to the coronary artery, bypassing the blocked section. The internal mammary artery is a reliable conduit for this procedure, as it is less prone to clogging than other graft options. This surgical intervention helps to alleviate symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain and shortness of breath, and can improve overall heart function.

Supplement Ulnar Nerve with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Open Approach
During this surgical procedure, a non-autologous tissue substitute is used to supplement the ulnar nerve, which is responsible for transmitting signals from the brain to the hand and arm. The open approach involves making an incision in the affected area, typically the elbow or wrist, to access the ulnar nerve. The tissue substitute is then implanted to support and reinforce the nerve, promoting healing and functional recovery. This procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as cubital tunnel syndrome, nerve damage, or compression, which can cause symptoms like numbness, tingling, and weakness in the hand and arm.

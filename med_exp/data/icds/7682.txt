Introduction of Nitric Oxide Gas into Respiratory Tract, Via Natural or Artificial Opening
This medical procedure involves the administration of nitric oxide gas into the respiratory tract through a natural or artificial opening, such as the nostrils or a tracheostomy tube. The gas is introduced to help relax the airway muscles, reduce inflammation, and improve oxygenation of the blood. The procedure is often performed in critical care settings, such as intensive care units, to treat respiratory distress syndrome or pulmonary hypertension. By delivering nitric oxide directly to the lungs, healthcare providers can help improve lung function and reduce the risk of respiratory failure.

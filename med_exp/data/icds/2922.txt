Excision of Omentum, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach, Diagnostic
Percutaneous endoscopic excision of the omentum is a minimally invasive surgical procedure that involves the removal of the omentum, a fold of peritoneum that attaches the stomach to the back of the abdominal wall, through a small incision using an endoscope. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia or conscious sedation to ensure patient comfort and relaxation. The endoscope, equipped with a camera and specialized instruments, is inserted through the skin and guided to the omentum, allowing the surgeon to visualize and excise the tissue. The diagnostic nature of this procedure enables the surgeon to examine the excised tissue for any abnormalities or diseases, providing valuable information for further treatment planning.

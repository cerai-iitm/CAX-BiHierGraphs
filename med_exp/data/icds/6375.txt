Extirpation of Matter from Left Ureter, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope to visually examine the left ureter and extract unwanted matter, such as stones or debris, through a natural or artificial opening. The endoscope, equipped with specialized instruments, is inserted through the urethra or a small incision to access the ureter, allowing the physician to carefully remove the obstructing material. This procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia to ensure patient comfort and is often used to treat conditions such as ureteral stones, hydronephrosis, or ureteropelvic junction obstruction. Following the procedure, patients may experience some pain, bruising, or bleeding, but these symptoms are usually manageable with medication and resolve on their own within a few days.

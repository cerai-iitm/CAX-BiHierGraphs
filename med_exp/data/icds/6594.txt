Insertion of Radioactive Element into Vagina, Via Natural or Artificial Opening
The insertion of a radioactive element into the vagina is a medical procedure used to target and treat cervical or vaginal cancer. This treatment involves placing a small amount of radioactive material, such as cesium or iridium, into the vagina through the natural opening or an artificial opening created during the procedure. The radioactive element is typically inserted using an applicator, which is guided by imaging techniques such as ultrasound or CT scans to ensure accurate placement. The goal of this treatment is to deliver localized radiation therapy to the tumor site, reducing the risk of damage to surrounding healthy tissue.

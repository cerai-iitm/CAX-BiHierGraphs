Excision of Epiglottis, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the removal of the epiglottis, a flap-like structure located at the entrance of the larynx, through an endoscopic approach, utilizing a flexible or rigid tube with a camera and light on the end to visualize the area. The procedure is typically performed through the mouth or nose, without the need for an external incision, and is often used for diagnostic purposes to identify any abnormalities or lesions in the epiglottis. During the procedure, the surgeon uses specialized instruments to carefully excise the epiglottis, taking care to avoid damaging surrounding tissue. The procedure may be performed under general anesthesia or local anesthesia with sedation, depending on the individual case.

Destruction of Conduction Mechanism, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the deliberate destruction of the conduction mechanism, which is responsible for transmitting abnormal electrical signals in the heart. Through a percutaneous approach, a catheter is inserted into a blood vessel in the leg or arm and guided to the heart, where it emits energy to ablate the targeted area. This eliminates the abnormal pathway, restoring a normal heart rhythm and alleviating symptoms such as rapid heartbeat, dizziness, and shortness of breath. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and is often used to treat conditions such as atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, and ventricular tachycardia.

Excision of Right Middle Lobe Bronchus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of the right middle lobe bronchus, a segment of the airway that divides into smaller passages, through a natural or artificial opening. The procedure is performed using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, which is inserted through the mouth or nose to visualize the airways. Under endoscopic guidance, the surgeon excises the affected bronchus, which may be necessary to diagnosis and treat conditions such as bronchial stenosis, tumors, or bleeding. The procedure requires general anesthesia and is usually performed in a hospital setting by a trained interventional pulmonologist or thoracic surgeon.

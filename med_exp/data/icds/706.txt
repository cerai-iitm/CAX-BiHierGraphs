Dilation of Right Internal Carotid Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of a balloon-tipped catheter to widen the narrowed or blocked right internal carotid artery, restoring blood flow to the brain and reducing the risk of stroke or transient ischemic attack. The catheter is inserted through a small incision in the groin area and guided through the arteries to the blockage site, where the balloon is inflated to dilate the artery. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and fluoroscopic guidance, allowing the healthcare provider to visualize the artery and navigate the catheter. By opening up the blocked artery, this procedure can help alleviate symptoms such as dizziness, blurred vision, and weakness in the face, arm, or leg.

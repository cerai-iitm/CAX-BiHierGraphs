Occlusion of Right Hepatic Duct with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the closure of the right hepatic duct using an intraluminal device, which is inserted through the skin under imaging guidance. The device is designed to block the flow of bile in the duct, and its placement is typically performed to treat conditions such as bile duct leaks or obstruction. During the procedure, a small incision is made in the skin, and a thin tube called a catheter is guided through the liver to the right hepatic duct. The intraluminal device is then inserted through the catheter and deployed within the duct to occlude it.

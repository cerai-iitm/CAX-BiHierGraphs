Drainage of Right Lower Lung Lobe, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During this minimally invasive procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, known as an endoscope, is inserted through the mouth or nose and guided through the airways to the right lower lung lobe. The scope enables the physician to visualize the lung tissue and locate the area that requires drainage. A small tube or instrument is then inserted through the scope to drain excess fluid, pus, or blood from the right lower lung lobe, helping to restore proper lung function and alleviate symptoms such as shortness of breath, chest pain, or coughing. The entire procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation, allowing the patient to remain comfortable and relaxed throughout the process.

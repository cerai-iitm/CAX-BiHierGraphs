Insertion of Neurostimulator Lead into Brain, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a neurostimulator lead into the brain through a percutaneous approach, typically under local anesthesia and fluoroscopic guidance. The lead is carefully placed to target specific areas of the brain, such as the thalamus or subthalamic nucleus, to deliver electrical impulses that can help manage chronic pain, tremors, or other movement disorders. The procedure usually takes around 1-2 hours to complete, and the patient may be required to remain awake during the procedure to provide feedback on the effectiveness of the stimulation. After the procedure, the lead is connected to a neurostimulator device, which is implanted under the skin, usually in the chest or abdomen, to regulate the electrical impulses.

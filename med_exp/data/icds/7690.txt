Introduction of Other Antineoplastic into Upper GI, Via Natural or Artificial Opening
This medical procedure involves the administration of cancer-fighting medications directly into the upper gastrointestinal (GI) tract through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth, esophagus, or a gastrostomy tube. The antineoplastic agents are introduced to target malignant cells and tumors in the upper GI tract, including the esophagus, stomach, and small intestine. This localized treatment aims to minimize systemic side effects and maximize the therapeutic benefits for patients with GI cancers. The procedure may be performed under endoscopic guidance to ensure accurate placement of the medication.

Dilation of Left Hepatic Duct with Intraluminal Device, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the use of an intraluminal device to dilate the left hepatic duct, a tubular structure that carries bile from the liver to the small intestine. The device is inserted through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth or a surgically created incision, to access the duct. Once in place, the device expands to widen the duct, allowing for improved bile flow and drainage. This procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as bile duct obstruction or narrowing, which can cause symptoms like jaundice, itching, and abdominal pain.

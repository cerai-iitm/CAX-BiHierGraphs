Insertion of Feeding Device into Stomach, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a feeding device, such as a percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) tube, into the stomach through a natural or artificial opening. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation, and the stomach is visualized using an endoscope to guide the placement of the feeding device. Once in place, the device allows for direct nutrition and medication administration, providing essential support for individuals who have difficulty eating orally. The endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications compared to an open surgical procedure, allowing for faster recovery and shorter hospital stays.

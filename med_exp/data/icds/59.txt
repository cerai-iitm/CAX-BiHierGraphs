Excision of Brain, Open Approach, Diagnostic
This invasive surgical procedure involves making an incision in the skull to access the brain, allowing for the removal of a portion of brain tissue or lesion for diagnostic purposes. The open approach requires a craniotomy, where a section of the skull is temporarily removed to expose the brain, and the excised tissue is then sent for further examination and testing. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and requires close monitoring of the patient's vital signs and neurological function. The diagnostic excision of brain tissue can help identify conditions such as tumors, cysts, or infectious diseases, and guide treatment decisions.

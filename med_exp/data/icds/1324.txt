Supplement Right External Iliac Artery with Synthetic Substitute, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves accessing the right external iliac artery through a small incision in the skin, typically in the groin area, using imaging guidance. A synthetic graft is then advanced through a catheter and placed in the artery to supplement blood flow and restore perfusion to the affected limb. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and is often used to treat peripheral artery disease, atherosclerosis, or other conditions that restrict blood flow to the legs. The percutaneous approach allows for faster recovery and less tissue trauma compared to open surgical techniques.

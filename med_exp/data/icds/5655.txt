Fusion of Occipital-cervical Joint with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the fusion of the occipital-cervical joint in the spine, where the base of the skull meets the neck, using an autologous tissue substitute, which is tissue taken from the patient's own body. The procedure is performed through a posterior approach, accessing the joint from the back, and involves an open approach, where a larger incision is made to visualize the anatomy. The goal of the procedure is to stabilize the joint and alleviate pain, numbness, or weakness in the arms, hands, or legs caused by conditions such as spinal stenosis or spinal instability. The autologous tissue substitute is used to promote bone growth and fusion, ultimately resulting in a more stable spine.

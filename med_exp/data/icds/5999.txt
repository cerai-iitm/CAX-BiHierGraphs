Insertion of Spacer into Right Hip Joint, Open Approach
During an open surgical procedure, a spacer is implanted into the right hip joint to provide temporary relief from severe hip pain and inflammation, often as a precursor to a total hip replacement. The spacer is typically made of antibiotic-impregnated cement and is designed to maintain joint space and alignment while treating underlying infections or promoting bone growth. The open approach allows for direct visualization of the joint and surrounding tissue, enabling the surgeon to accurately position the spacer and address any related anatomical issues. Following implantation, the spacer helps to reduce symptoms and improve mobility, allowing the patient to regain functional ability and quality of life.

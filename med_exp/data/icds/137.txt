Insertion of Infusion Device into Spinal Canal, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the percutaneous insertion of an infusion device into the spinal canal, allowing for the delivery of medication or other therapeutic agents directly to the spine. The infusion device is inserted through the skin and into the spinal canal, where it can remain in place for an extended period of time. The device is designed to provide a steady, controlled flow of medication to the area, which can help to manage pain, inflammation, or other conditions. This minimally invasive procedure can be performed under local anesthesia and is often used to treat conditions such as chronic pain, cancer pain, or spasticity.

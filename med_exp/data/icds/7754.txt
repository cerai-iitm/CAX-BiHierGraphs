Irrigation of Respiratory Tract using Irrigating Substance, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the use of an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, to visualize the respiratory tract and facilitate irrigation. A sterile irrigating substance is introduced through the endoscope to flush out debris, mucus, or other foreign particles from the respiratory tract. This diagnostic procedure is performed through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth or nose, or through a tracheostomy tube. The goal of the irrigation is to aid in the diagnosis of respiratory conditions, such as pneumonia or bronchitis, by allowing for a clear view of the tract and collection of samples for further examination.

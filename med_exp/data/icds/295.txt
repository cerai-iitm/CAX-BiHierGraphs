Bypass Coronary Artery, Two Arteries from Coronary Artery with Autologous Venous Tissue, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves creating a detour around blocked or narrowed sections of two coronary arteries, restoring blood flow to the heart by using a portion of the patient's own vein, harvested from another part of the body, as a graft. The open approach requires a sternotomy, where the chest is opened to access the heart, allowing the surgeon to visualize the coronary arteries and perform the bypass grafting. The autologous venous tissue, typically taken from the leg, is connected to the blocked coronary artery, creating a new path for blood to flow to the heart, reducing symptoms of coronary artery disease. By rerouting blood flow around the blockages, this procedure aims to improve cardiac function and alleviate symptoms such as chest pain and shortness of breath.

Transfusion of Nonautologous Red Blood Cells into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
A medical professional inserts a small needle into a peripheral vein, typically in the arm or hand, to administer nonautologous red blood cells into the bloodstream. This percutaneous approach allows for a direct and efficient transfer of the donated red blood cells, helping to restore oxygen delivery to the body's tissues. The transfusion process is closely monitored to ensure the patient's safety and to prevent any potential complications. The goal of this procedure is to treat anemia, hemorrhage, or other conditions where red blood cell levels are low, promoting healthy oxygenation and improving overall health outcomes.

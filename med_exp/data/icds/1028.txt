Dilation of Right Common Iliac Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of a balloon-tipped catheter to dilate the narrowed or blocked right common iliac artery, restoring blood flow to the lower extremities. The catheter is inserted through a small incision in the groin, guided by X-ray or ultrasound, and advanced to the site of the blockage. Once in place, the balloon is inflated, gently pushing the arterial walls outward to widen the vessel, and then deflated and removed. The procedure can help to alleviate symptoms such as leg pain, cramping, and weakness, and improve overall circulation.

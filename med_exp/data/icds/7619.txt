Introduction of Other Hormone into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
A minimally invasive procedure is performed to introduce a hormone into a peripheral vein, typically in the arm or hand, through a percutaneous approach, which involves making a small incision in the skin. The hormone is administered through a thin catheter or needle inserted into the vein, allowing for targeted delivery of the medication. This procedure is often used to treat hormone deficiencies or imbalances, such as thyroid or growth hormone deficiencies. The percutaneous approach minimizes trauma to the surrounding tissue and reduces the risk of complications, promoting a faster recovery.

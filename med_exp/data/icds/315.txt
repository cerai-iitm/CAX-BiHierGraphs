Bypass Coronary Artery, Four or More Arteries from Coronary Artery with Autologous Venous Tissue, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves bypassing four or more blocked coronary arteries by grafting a section of healthy autologous vein, taken from the patient's own body, to restore normal blood flow to the heart. The open approach requires a sternotomy, where the chest is opened to access the heart, allowing the surgeon to visually inspect the coronary arteries and perform the bypass grafting. The autologous venous tissue is typically harvested from the patient's leg or arm, and then grafted onto the blocked coronary arteries, creating a new pathway for oxygen-rich blood to flow to the heart. The goal of this complex procedure is to relieve symptoms of coronary artery disease, improve heart function, and reduce the risk of heart attack.

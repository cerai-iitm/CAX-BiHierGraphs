Excision of Right Vitreous, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous excision of the right vitreous is a minimally invasive surgical procedure where a small incision is made in the sclera, the white part of the eye, to access the vitreous humor. The vitreous humor is the clear, gel-like substance that fills the center of the eye, giving it its shape and helping to maintain its pressure. Through the incision, a specialized instrument is inserted to carefully remove the vitreous humor, which can be clouded or damaged due to various eye conditions, such as vitreomacular adhesion or retinal detachment. The goal of this procedure is to improve vision by removing the cloudy or damaged vitreous humor and allowing the eye to regain its clarity and function.

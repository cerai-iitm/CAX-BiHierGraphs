Excision of Middle Esophagus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the removal of a portion of the middle esophagus using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, through a natural or artificial opening. The endoscope is guided through the mouth or a small incision in the abdomen to reach the affected area, allowing the surgeon to visualize the esophagus and excise the targeted tissue. This diagnostic procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may be used to diagnose and treat conditions such as esophageal cancer, Barrett's esophagus, or high-grade dysplasia. The excised tissue is then sent to a laboratory for further examination and diagnosis.

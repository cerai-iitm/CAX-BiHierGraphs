Replacement of Abdomen Skin with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Partial Thickness, External Approach
This surgical procedure involves replacing a partial thickness portion of the abdomen skin with an autologous tissue substitute, which is a tissue graft taken from the patient's own body, harvested from a different area, and transplanted to the affected area. The external approach is used, where the surgeon makes an incision in the skin to access the underlying tissue, and then carefully implants the substitute tissue to promote healing and tissue regeneration. The autologous tissue substitute is used to repair damaged or defect skin, promoting a more natural and durable repair. This procedure is often performed to treat complex skin defects, such as those resulting from trauma, burns, or surgical wounds.

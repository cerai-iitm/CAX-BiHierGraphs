Extirpation of Matter from Coronary Artery, One Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This percutaneous procedure involves the removal of unwanted material, such as plaque or clot, from a single coronary artery through a minimally invasive approach. The procedure is typically performed under fluoroscopic guidance, allowing the interventional cardiologist to visualize the coronary arteries and precisely target the affected area. A specialized catheter is inserted through a small incision in the groin or arm, and advanced to the blockage in the coronary artery, where the offending material is then extracted or aspirated. By restoring blood flow to the affected area, this procedure can help to alleviate symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain or shortness of breath.

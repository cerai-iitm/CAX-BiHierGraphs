Fusion of Occipital-cervical Joint with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the fusion of the occipital-cervical joint, stabilizing the connection between the base of the skull and the cervical spine. The posterior approach is used, where the surgeon accesses the joint from the back of the head and neck. A nonautologous tissue substitute, such as a bone graft or artificial material, is implanted to facilitate bone growth and fusion. The open approach requires an incision in the skin and tissues to expose the joint, allowing the surgeon to directly visualize and access the area.

Replacement of Aortic Valve with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves replacing the diseased or damaged aortic valve with a valve made from the patient's own tissue, obtained from a different part of the body, through a small hole in the skin. The autologous tissue substitute is delivered through a catheter inserted in the leg or arm, guided by imaging technologies and advanced navigation systems. Once in place, the new valve is expanded using a balloon, allowing it to take over the function of the damaged valve and restore normal blood flow. This percutaneous approach minimizes surgical trauma and promotes faster recovery, reducing the need for open-heart surgery and lengthy hospital stays.

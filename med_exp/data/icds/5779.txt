Removal of Synthetic Substitute from Left Metacarpophalangeal Joint, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the open removal of a synthetic substitute, such as a joint spacer or implant, from the left metacarpophalangeal joint, which connects the hand bones to the fingers. The surgeon makes an incision in the skin and soft tissue to access the joint, carefully dissects the synthetic material from surrounding tissue and bone, and then extracts it from the joint space. The goal of this procedure is to relieve symptoms caused by the synthetic substitute, which may include pain, stiffness, and limited range of motion. After removal, the joint may be reconstructed or repaired to restore normal function and mobility.

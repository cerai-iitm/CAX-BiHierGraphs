Dilation of Portal Vein, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous portal vein dilation is a minimally invasive procedure that uses image guidance to access the portal vein through the skin. A balloon catheter is then inserted through the access site and advanced to the narrowed or blocked area of the portal vein, where it is inflated to widen the vein and improve blood flow. This procedure is often performed to treat portal hypertension, a condition characterized by high blood pressure in the portal vein, which can lead to complications such as variceal bleeding and ascites. The goal of the procedure is to reduce portal vein pressure and alleviate symptoms, improving overall liver function and patient outcomes.

Drainage of Left Lower Lobe Bronchus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a flexible tube with a camera and lighting through the mouth or nose to reach the left lower lobe bronchus. The endoscope allows the physician to visualize the airway and identify the affected area. A specialized instrument is then used to aspirate or drain excess mucus, pus, or other fluids from the left lower lobe bronchus, helping to restore airflow and alleviate respiratory distress. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia, ensuring the patient's comfort and safety throughout the process.

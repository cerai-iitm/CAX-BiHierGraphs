Introduction of Anti-inflammatory into Respiratory Tract, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This medical procedure involves the insertion of an anti-inflammatory medication directly into the respiratory tract through an endoscope, which is a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end. The endoscope is guided through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth or a tracheostomy tube, to access the respiratory tract. The anti-inflammatory medication is then delivered locally to the affected area, allowing for targeted treatment and relief from inflammation and swelling. This minimally invasive procedure is often used to treat respiratory conditions such as asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and bronchitis.

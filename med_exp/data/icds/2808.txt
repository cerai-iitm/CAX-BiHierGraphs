Excision of Esophagus, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the removal of all or part of the esophagus through a percutaneous endoscopic approach, utilizing small incisions and an endoscope to visualize the internal structures. The procedure requires general anesthesia and is typically performed to treat esophageal cancer, benign tumors, or severe esophageal trauma. During the procedure, the surgeon uses an endoscope to guide the removal of the affected portion of the esophagus, and the remaining esophageal tissue is then reconnected to restore swallowing function. Post-operative care includes careful monitoring for complications and a gradual return to oral nutrition.

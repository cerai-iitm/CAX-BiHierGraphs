Dilation of Coronary Artery, Two Arteries with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This percutaneous coronary intervention involves the use of a drug-eluting intraluminal device to dilate two coronary arteries that have become narrowed or blocked, restricting blood flow to the heart. The procedure is performed through small incisions in the skin, and a catheter is guided through the arteries to the blockages, where the device is deployed to open up the arteries and restore blood flow. The drug-eluting feature of the device helps to prevent the arteries from narrowing again by slowly releasing medication over time. By opening up multiple blocked arteries, this procedure can improve blood flow to the heart, reducing symptoms such as chest pain and shortness of breath.

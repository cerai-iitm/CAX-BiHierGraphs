Removal of Other Device from Respiratory Tract, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This procedure involves the removal of a foreign object or device from the respiratory tract, which includes the nose, mouth, throat, and lungs, using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, inserted through a natural or artificial opening, such as the mouth or a tracheostomy tube. The endoscope allows the physician to visually examine the respiratory tract and retrieve the device with specialized instruments, minimizing trauma to surrounding tissues. The procedure is typically performed under sedation or local anesthesia to ensure patient comfort. The removal of the device can help restore normal breathing function and alleviate symptoms such as coughing, wheezing, or difficulty swallowing.

Occlusion of Left Atrial Appendage, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the intentional blocking or closure of the left atrial appendage, a small ear-shaped sac in the upper chamber of the heart, through an open approach. The goal is to prevent stroke by eliminating the risk of blood clots forming in the appendage and traveling to the brain. The procedure is typically performed in patients with atrial fibrillation, a type of irregular heartbeat, who are at high risk of stroke. The surgeon makes an incision in the chest to access the heart and then occludes the left atrial appendage using specialized instruments or devices.

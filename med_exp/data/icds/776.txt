Extirpation of Matter from Right Internal Carotid Artery, Open Approach
During this surgical procedure, a surgeon makes an incision in the neck to access the right internal carotid artery, a major blood vessel supplying oxygenated blood to the brain. The goal of the operation is to remove unwanted matter, such as a blood clot or plaque, that is blocking or obstructing the artery, which can cause stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) if left untreated. Using specialized instruments and techniques, the surgeon carefully dissects the artery to locate and extract the obstructing material, taking care to avoid damaging the delicate surrounding tissues. Once the blockage is removed, the artery is closed, and the incision site is sutured.

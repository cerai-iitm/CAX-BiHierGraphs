Occlusion of Right Uterine Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a catheter through the skin and guiding it to the right uterine artery to deploy an intraluminal device that blocks blood flow, thereby occluding the artery. The percutaneous approach allows for a small incision and reduces recovery time. The device is designed to remain in place permanently, and its placement is typically performed under fluoroscopic guidance to ensure accurate positioning. The occlusion of the right uterine artery is often used to treat conditions such as uterine fibroids, adenomyosis, or other abnormalities that cause excessive bleeding or pain.

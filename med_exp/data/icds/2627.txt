Bypass Ileum to Cutaneous, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This surgical procedure involves creating a bypass between the ileum, a part of the small intestine, and the skin, allowing for the diversion of intestinal contents to a stoma on the abdomen. The procedure is performed percutaneously, meaning through the skin, with the aid of an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, which guides the surgeon through the process. The bypass is created to relieve obstruction or blockage in the small intestine, allowing for the patient to pass stool and fluids through the stoma instead. This procedure is often performed in emergency situations, such as bowel obstruction or perforation, to alleviate life-threatening complications.

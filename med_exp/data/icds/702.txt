Dilation of Right Internal Carotid Artery with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves making a small incision in the groin area to access the right internal carotid artery, which supplies blood to the brain. A catheter is then guided through the incision and advanced to the blocked or narrowed artery, where a drug-eluting intraluminal device is placed to widen the artery and improve blood flow. The device is designed to release medication over time to prevent the artery from narrowing again and reduce the risk of future blockages. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and imaging guidance to minimize risks and ensure accurate placement of the device.

Occlusion of Splenic Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the percutaneous insertion of an intraluminal device to occlude the splenic artery, thereby blocking blood flow to the spleen. The procedure is typically performed under imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, to accurately position the device. The intraluminal device is designed to expand within the artery, causing a blockage that prevents blood from flowing through the splenic artery. This procedure is often used to treat various conditions, including splenic artery aneurysms, splenic artery pseudoaneurysms, and hypersplenism.

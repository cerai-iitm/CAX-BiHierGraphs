Dilation of Coronary Artery, Two Arteries, Bifurcation, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves widening two coronary arteries at the point where they branch off from each other, known as the bifurcation site, using a percutaneous approach. A small incision is made in the skin and a catheter is inserted through an artery in the leg or arm, guided by X-ray or ultrasound to the blocked coronary arteries. A small balloon is then inflated to compress the blockage and widen the artery, improving blood flow to the heart. The goal of this procedure is to relieve symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain or shortness of breath, and reduce the risk of heart attack.

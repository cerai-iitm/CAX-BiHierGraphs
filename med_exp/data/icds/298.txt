Bypass Coronary Artery, Two Arteries from Coronary Artery with Autologous Arterial Tissue, Open Approach
This procedure involves the creation of a bypass graft to restore blood flow to the heart by connecting two coronary arteries using a section of the patient's own artery, typically the left internal mammary artery, to divert blood flow around a blockage or narrowing. The open approach requires a sternotomy, a surgical incision in the chest, to access the coronary arteries. The autologous arterial tissue, harvested from the patient's own body, is grafted to the affected coronary arteries, allowing blood to flow around the blockage and re-establishing normal blood flow to the heart. This complex surgical procedure is typically performed to treat severe coronary artery disease, often in individuals who have not responded to other treatments.

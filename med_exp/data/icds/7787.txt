Measurement of Cardiac Rhythm, External Approach
This procedure involves the use of external devices to record and analyze the electrical activity of the heart, providing valuable information about the heart's rhythm and functioning. Electrodes are placed on the skin, typically on the chest, to detect the heart's electrical signals, which are then transmitted to a monitoring device. The recorded data is used to identify any abnormalities in the heart's rhythm, such as arrhythmias or tachycardias, and to guide treatment decisions. The measurement of cardiac rhythm through external approaches is a non-invasive and painless procedure that provides essential diagnostic information for cardiac healthcare providers.

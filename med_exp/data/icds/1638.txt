Occlusion of Superior Mesenteric Vein with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an intraluminal device to obstruct the flow of blood in the superior mesenteric vein, a vital vessel that supplies oxygenated blood to the small intestine. The device is inserted through a small incision in the skin, guided by imaging technology, and placed within the vein to block it. This procedure may be performed to treat various conditions, including portal hypertension, variceal bleeding, or to divert blood flow from a damaged or diseased section of the vein. The occlusion of the superior mesenteric vein can help to reduce bleeding, alleviate symptoms, and improve overall cardiovascular health.

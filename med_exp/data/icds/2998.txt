Insertion of Feeding Device into Jejunum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a feeding device, such as a jejunal feeding tube, through the mouth or a pre-existing opening, guided by an endoscope to access the jejunum. The jejunum is a part of the small intestine, and direct feeding into this area can provide essential nutrition to patients who have difficulty swallowing or absorbing nutrients through the stomach. Under endoscopic visualization, a guidewire is advanced through the working channel of the endoscope, and the feeding device is then inserted over the guidewire into the jejunum. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia to minimize discomfort and ensure safe placement of the feeding device.

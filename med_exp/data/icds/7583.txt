Transfusion of Allogeneic Unrelated Hematopoietic Stem Cells into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cells from an unrelated donor are infused into the central venous system through a percutaneous approach, allowing for the transplantation of healthy stem cells to replace damaged or diseased cells. This procedure is typically performed in patients with hematologic malignancies or other blood disorders, with the goal of restoring normal blood cell production and immune function. The transfusion is usually done through a central venous catheter inserted into a large vein in the chest, and the stem cells are administered over a period of several hours. Following the procedure, the patient is closely monitored for any signs of complications or graft-versus-host disease.

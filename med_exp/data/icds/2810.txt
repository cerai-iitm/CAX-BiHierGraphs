Excision of Esophagus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of the esophagus through a natural or artificial opening, utilizing a flexible tube with a camera and specialized instruments to visualize and excise the affected esophageal tissue. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may be done to treat conditions such as esophageal cancer, benign tumors, or severe esophageal damage. The use of endoscopy allows for a minimally invasive approach, reducing the risk of complications and promoting faster recovery. The removed esophagus is then sent to a laboratory for further examination to determine the extent of the disease or condition.

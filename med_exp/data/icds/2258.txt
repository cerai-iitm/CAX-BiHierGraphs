Excision of Diaphragm, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves making an incision in the chest or abdominal wall to access the diaphragm, a dome-shaped muscle that separates the chest cavity from the abdominal cavity. The surgeon then carefully excises, or removes, a portion of the diaphragm to treat conditions such as diaphragmatic hernia, tumor, or trauma. The open approach allows for direct visualization of the diaphragm and surrounding tissues, enabling the surgeon to precisely identify and remove the affected area. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may require a hospital stay for post-operative recovery and monitoring.

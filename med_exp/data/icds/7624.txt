Introduction of Low-dose Interleukin-2 into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the percutaneous insertion of a needle into a central vein, typically in the chest or arm, to administer a low dose of interleukin-2, a type of immunotherapy that stimulates the immune system to fight cancer. Under local anesthesia and image guidance, a thin catheter is advanced through the needle to deliver the medication directly into the central vein. The procedure is usually performed in an outpatient setting and may require repeated administrations to achieve optimal results. Close monitoring is necessary to mitigate potential side effects, such as flu-like symptoms and injection site reactions.

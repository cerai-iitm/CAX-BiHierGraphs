Dilation of Left Subclavian Artery with Two Intraluminal Devices, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of two intraluminal devices through the skin to dilate the left subclavian artery, which supplies blood to the arm and chest. The percutaneous approach allows the physician to access the artery through a small puncture in the skin, reducing the risk of complications and promoting faster healing. Once the devices are in place, they expand to widen the narrowed or blocked artery, restoring blood flow to the affected area. This procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as atherosclerosis, stenosis, or thrombosis.

Measurement of Cardiac Rhythm, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the insertion of an electrode through the skin to record the electrical activity of the heart, allowing for the measurement of cardiac rhythm. The percutaneous approach enables real-time monitoring of the heart's electrical impulses, which helps diagnose and manage various heart rhythm disorders, such as arrhythmias, atrial fibrillation, and ventricular tachycardia. The electrode is typically placed through a small incision in the chest, and the procedure is usually performed under local anesthesia to minimize discomfort. The recorded data is then analyzed to identify any irregularities in the heart's rhythm, guiding further treatment and management.

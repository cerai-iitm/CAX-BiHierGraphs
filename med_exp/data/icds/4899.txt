Reposition Maxilla with Internal Fixation Device, Percutaneous Approach
This surgical procedure involves repositioning the maxilla, the upper jawbone, to correct facial deformities or malocclusion, using a percutaneous approach that minimizes incisions and promotes healing. The procedure is performed under general anesthesia, and the surgeon makes small incisions in the skin to access the maxilla and reposition it to the desired location. An internal fixation device, such as plates and screws, is then used to stabilize the bone in its new position, allowing for proper healing and alignment. The procedure can improve facial aesthetics, restore proper oral function, and alleviate associated symptoms such as headaches and chewing difficulties.

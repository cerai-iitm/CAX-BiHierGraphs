Restriction of Esophagogastric Junction, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope inserted through the mouth to reach the esophagogastric junction, where a restrictive device or implant is placed to narrow the junction and prevent stomach contents from flowing back up into the esophagus. The goal of the procedure is to treat gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and its symptoms, such as heartburn and regurgitation. The percutaneous endoscopic approach allows for a less invasive and less painful treatment option compared to traditional surgical methods. The restrictive device or implant helps to restore the natural barrier between the esophagus and stomach, providing relief from chronic symptoms and improving quality of life.

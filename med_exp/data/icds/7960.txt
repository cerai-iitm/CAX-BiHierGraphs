Ultrasonography of Right Heart
This non-invasive imaging test uses high-frequency sound waves to produce detailed images of the right heart, allowing healthcare providers to evaluate its structure, function, and blood flow. During the procedure, a handheld probe called a transducer is placed on the chest, which emits ultrasound waves that bounce off the heart and return to the transducer, generating images on a screen. These images help diagnose and monitor various conditions affecting the right heart, such as cardiomyopathy, heart failure, and pulmonary hypertension. The test is usually performed in a doctor's office or hospital, and it does not expose the patient to ionizing radiation.

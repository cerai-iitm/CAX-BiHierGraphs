Fluoroscopy of Hepatic Artery using Other Contrast
During this procedure, a specialized X-ray machine called a fluoroscope is used to produce real-time images of the hepatic artery and its surrounding blood vessels. A contrast agent, different from the commonly used iodine-based dyes, is injected into the artery to enhance visibility. The fluoroscope guides the injection of the contrast material, allowing the radiologist to observe the flow of blood and identify any abnormalities, such as blockages or narrowing, in the hepatic artery. This procedure is typically performed to diagnose and guide treatment of liver-related conditions, including liver cancer, cirrhosis, and liver failure.

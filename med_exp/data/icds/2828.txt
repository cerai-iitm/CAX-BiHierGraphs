Excision of Small Intestine, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the removal of a portion of the small intestine through a small incision using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, to visualize the area. The scope is inserted through the skin and guided to the affected area, allowing the surgeon to pinpoint the exact location of the diseased or damaged section of intestine. Once located, specialized instruments are used to excise the segment of intestine, and the area is then closed, usually with sutures or staples. The procedure is often performed under general anesthesia and typically requires a shorter hospital stay compared to traditional open surgery.

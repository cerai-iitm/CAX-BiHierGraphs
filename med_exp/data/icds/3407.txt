Excision of Left Hepatic Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of a portion of the left hepatic duct, which is a tube that carries bile from the liver to the small intestine, through a natural or artificial opening. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and treat conditions such as bile duct stones, strictures, or cancer that are blocking the flow of bile. During the procedure, an endoscope is inserted through the mouth or nose and guided through the digestive tract to the location of the left hepatic duct, where specialized instruments are used to excise the affected portion. The procedure is typically performed under sedation or general anesthesia to minimize discomfort and ensure optimal results.

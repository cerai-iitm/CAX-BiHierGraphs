Dilation of Superior Mesenteric Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a small balloon-tipped catheter through a tiny incision in the skin and guiding it to the superior mesenteric vein under fluoroscopic guidance. The balloon is then inflated to dilate the narrowed or blocked segment of the vein, restoring blood flow and relieving symptoms. The procedure is typically performed to treat chronic mesenteric ischemia, a condition characterized by recurring abdominal pain due to inadequate blood supply to the small intestine. The percutaneous approach minimizes tissue trauma and allows for faster recovery compared to open surgery.

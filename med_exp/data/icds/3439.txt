Extirpation of Matter from Right Hepatic Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of unwanted material from the right hepatic duct, which is a vital part of the biliary system that drains bile from the liver into the small intestine. Through a natural or artificial opening, an endoscope is inserted to access the duct and extract the obstructing matter, restoring normal bile flow and alleviating symptoms. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia, and the endoscope is equipped with specialized instruments to facilitate the extirpation process. The goal of this intervention is to alleviate jaundice, abdominal pain, and other complications associated with biliary obstruction.

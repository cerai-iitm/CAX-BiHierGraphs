Insertion of Pacemaker Lead into Right Ventricle, Percutaneous Approach
During this minimally invasive procedure, a pacemaker lead is inserted through the skin and into a vein, typically in the chest, guided by fluoroscopy or ultrasound. The lead is then advanced through the vein and into the right ventricle, a lower chamber of the heart, where it is positioned to regulate the heart's rhythm. Once in place, the lead is secured and connected to a pacemaker, a small device that emits electrical impulses to stimulate the heart to beat at a normal rate. This procedure is typically performed to treat abnormal heart rhythms, such as bradycardia, and is usually done under local anesthesia.

Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Peripheral Artery, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous introduction of a therapeutic substance into a peripheral artery is a minimally invasive procedure that involves inserting a small catheter or needle through the skin and into the affected artery. The therapeutic substance, such as a medication or contrast agent, is then delivered directly into the artery to treat a range of vascular conditions, including peripheral artery disease, vasospasm, and thrombosis. The procedure is typically performed under fluoroscopic guidance to ensure accurate placement of the catheter and effective delivery of the therapeutic substance. This approach offers a safer and less painful alternative to open surgical procedures, with faster recovery times and reduced risk of complications.

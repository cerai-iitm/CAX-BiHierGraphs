Computerized Tomography (CT Scan) of Right Pulmonary Artery using Other Contrast
A computerized tomography (CT) scan of the right pulmonary artery is a non-invasive imaging test that uses X-rays and computer technology to produce detailed cross-sectional images of the artery. During the procedure, a contrast agent other than iodine is injected into a vein in the arm to highlight the pulmonary artery and its surrounding tissues. The CT scanner rotates around the body, capturing images of the artery from multiple angles, which are then reconstructed by a computer to create detailed 3D images. These images help healthcare providers diagnose and monitor conditions affecting the right pulmonary artery, such as pulmonary embolism or aneurysm.

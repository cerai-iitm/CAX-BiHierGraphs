Insertion of Feeding Device into Duodenum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a feeding device, such as a duodenal tube, through the mouth or a previously created opening, guided by an endoscope to reach the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine. The device is placed through the mouth or an artificial opening, such as a gastrostomy, under endoscopic visualization to ensure accurate placement. The feeding device is designed to provide nutrition directly into the small intestine, bypassing the stomach, and is often used for patients who have difficulty swallowing or have an obstructed stomach. The endoscopic approach allows for a minimally invasive and relatively quick procedure, reducing the risk of complications and promoting faster recovery.

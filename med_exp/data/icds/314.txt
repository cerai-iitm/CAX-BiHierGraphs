Bypass Coronary Artery, Three Arteries from Left Internal Mammary, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves bypassing three blocked coronary arteries by grafting them with the left internal mammary artery, a vessel originating from the subclavian artery, to restore blood flow to the heart. The open approach requires a sternotomy, where the surgeon makes an incision in the chest to access the heart and perform the bypass grafting. The internal mammary artery is harvested and divided, then connected to the blocked coronary arteries, creating a detour for blood to flow around the blockages. By re-establishing blood flow, this procedure helps to alleviate symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain and shortness of breath.

Extraction of Common Bile Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of bile duct stones or other obstructions from the common bile duct through a natural or artificial opening, allowing for diagnostic evaluation and potential treatment of conditions such as gallstones, cholangitis, or pancreatitis. Using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, the healthcare provider visualizes the bile duct and extracts the obstruction, facilitating the flow of bile from the liver and gallbladder into the small intestine. The procedure is typically performed under moderate sedation and local anesthesia, and may be done in conjunction with other diagnostic tests such as ERCP or cholangiography. Successful extraction can alleviate symptoms such as abdominal pain, jaundice, and fever, and may prevent further complications.

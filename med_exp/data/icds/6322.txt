Drainage of Bladder, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, to drain the bladder through a natural or artificial opening. The endoscope is inserted through the urethra or a previously created opening, allowing the healthcare provider to visualize the bladder and drain excess fluid or debris. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and is often used to diagnose and treat conditions such as bladder obstruction, urinary retention, or urinary tract infections. The endoscopic approach allows for minimal tissue disruption and promotes faster healing and recovery.

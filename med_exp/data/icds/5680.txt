Fusion of 2 or more Cervical Vertebral Joints with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the fusion of two or more cervical vertebral joints utilizing non-biological tissue substitutes, such as bone grafts or ceramic spacers, to stabilize the spine. The surgeon approaches the affected area from the back, through an open incision, and focuses on the posterior column of the spine. The artificial tissue substitutes are carefully placed to promote bone growth and fusion between the targeted vertebrae, alleviating pressure on surrounding nerves and alleviating symptoms such as pain and stiffness. Over time, the substituted tissues stimulate new bone growth, resulting in a stable and strengthened spine.

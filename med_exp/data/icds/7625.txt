Introduction of Other Antineoplastic into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the percutaneous introduction of an antineoplastic agent into a central vein, typically through a needle or cannula, to treat cancer. The central vein is accessed through the skin, allowing for the direct administration of the medication into the bloodstream. This approach allows for a more targeted and efficient delivery of the antineoplastic agent, reducing systemic side effects and improving treatment outcomes. The procedure is usually performed under imaging guidance, such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy, to ensure accurate placement of the needle and medication.

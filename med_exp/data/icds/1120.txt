Dilation of Right Posterior Tibial Artery, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous dilation of the right posterior tibial artery is a minimally invasive procedure where a small incision is made in the skin to access the blocked artery. A small balloon-tipped catheter is then inserted through the incision and guided to the blockage using fluoroscopy or ultrasound. Once in place, the balloon is inflated to compress the plaque and widen the artery, restoring blood flow to the lower leg. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and has a low risk of complications, making it an attractive option for patients with peripheral artery disease.

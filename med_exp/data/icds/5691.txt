Fusion of Cervicothoracic Vertebral Joint with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the fusion of vertebrae in the cervicothoracic region, which connects the neck and upper back, to stabilize the spine and alleviate pain or discomfort. The procedure is performed through a posterior approach, meaning the incision is made in the back, and an open approach, which allows for direct visualization of the spine. A nonautologous tissue substitute, such as bone graft or synthetic material, is used to facilitate the fusion process, promoting the growth of new bone and stabilizing the vertebral joint. The posterior column of the spine is the specific area targeted in this procedure, which provides additional support and structure to the spine.

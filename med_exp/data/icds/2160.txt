Drainage of Left Pleura, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the insertion of a drainage catheter through the skin and into the left pleural space, a region between the lungs and chest wall, to remove excess fluid or air that has accumulated due to various medical conditions such as pneumonia, cancer, or heart failure. A guided imaging technique, such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy, is used to direct the catheter to the correct location. Once in place, the catheter is connected to a drainage system, allowing for the removal of the unwanted fluid or air, which can help to alleviate symptoms such as shortness of breath, chest pain, and coughing. The procedure is typically performed by a trained healthcare professional, such as a radiologist or pulmonologist, in a hospital or outpatient setting.

Fusion of 2 or more Cervical Vertebral Joints with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
Here is a detailed description of the procedure:

This surgical procedure involves fusing two or more cervical vertebral joints using an autologous tissue substitute, which is a graft taken from the patient's own body. The procedure is performed through a posterior approach, meaning the surgeon accesses the spine from the back of the neck, and an open approach, which involves making an incision in the skin to visualize the spine. During the procedure, the autologous tissue substitute is inserted into the disk space to promote bone growth and stabilize the spine. The goal of the fusion is to relieve pain and restore mobility by eliminating motion between the affected vertebrae.

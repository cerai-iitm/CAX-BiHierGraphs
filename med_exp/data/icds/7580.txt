Transfusion of Autologous Hematopoietic Stem Cells into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
Autologous hematopoietic stem cells are collected from the patient's own blood or bone marrow and then infused through a central vein using a percutaneous approach, a minimally invasive procedure where a needle is inserted through the skin. This procedure is often performed after high-dose chemotherapy or radiation therapy to restore the patient's immune system. The stem cells can differentiate into various blood cells, including red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets, to replenish the patient's blood supply. The goal of this treatment is to promote hematopoietic recovery and reduce the risk of complications associated with blood disorders or cancer treatment.

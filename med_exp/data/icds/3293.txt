Dilation of Right Hepatic Duct with Intraluminal Device, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of an intraluminal device through a natural or artificial opening to dilate the right hepatic duct, which is a part of the bile duct system that carries bile from the liver to the small intestine. The goal of this procedure is to widen the duct and relieve blockages or strictures that may be preventing the proper flow of bile. During the procedure, the endoscope is guided through the mouth or a surgical opening to access the bile duct system, and then the intraluminal device is inserted to dilate the narrowed or blocked area. The procedure can help to restore bile flow, alleviate symptoms such as jaundice and abdominal pain, and improve overall liver function.

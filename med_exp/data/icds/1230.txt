Occlusion of Hepatic Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the insertion of a small device through the skin to block the flow of blood in the hepatic artery, which supplies oxygenated blood to the liver. The device is guided to the target site using imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, and once in place, it expands to occlude the artery, preventing blood flow. The percutaneous approach allows for a minimally invasive procedure, resulting in less trauma to the patient and a shorter recovery time. The goal of this procedure is to treat liver cancer, liver cirrhosis, or other conditions where reducing blood flow to the liver is beneficial.

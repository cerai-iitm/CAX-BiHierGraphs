Occlusion of Intracranial Artery with Extraluminal Device, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the placement of an extraluminal device to block the flow of blood through an intracranial artery, performed through an open approach, which requires a craniotomy to access the targeted blood vessel. The device is positioned outside the artery, but within the surrounding tissue, to achieve occlusion. The procedure is typically performed to treat conditions such as aneurysms, arteriovenous malformations, or fistulas, where blood flow needs to be redirected or stopped. By occluding the affected artery, the risk of bleeding or rupture is significantly reduced, and normal blood flow can be restored to surrounding brain tissue.

Release Omentum, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light, to visualize the omentum, a fatty membrane that surrounds the abdominal organs. Through a small incision in the skin, the endoscope is inserted to release the omentum, which can become adhered to other tissues or organs, causing pain, inflammation, or impaired organ function. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia or local anesthesia with sedation, and the endoscope guides the release of the omentum, allowing for minimal trauma to surrounding tissues. The goal of the procedure is to restore normal anatomy and alleviate symptoms, promoting improved digestive function and overall well-being.

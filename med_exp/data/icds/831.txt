Occlusion of Left External Carotid Artery with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an intraluminal device to occlude the left external carotid artery, which is accessed through a small incision in the skin. Under fluoroscopic guidance, a catheter is inserted through the femoral artery in the groin and guided to the targeted artery, where the device is deployed to block blood flow. The procedure is typically performed to treat conditions such as carotid body tumor or carotid artery aneurysm, and may be done in conjunction with other interventions such as embolization or surgical resection. The percutaneous approach minimizes tissue damage and promotes faster recovery compared to open surgical methods.

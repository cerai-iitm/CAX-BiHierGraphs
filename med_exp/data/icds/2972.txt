Extraction of Transverse Colon, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of a section of the transverse colon, which is the middle part of the large intestine, through a natural or artificial opening. The process is performed using an endoscope, a thin, flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, to visually examine the colon and facilitate the extraction. The procedure is typically done for diagnostic purposes, such as to collect tissue samples for biopsy or to investigate symptoms like abdominal pain or bleeding. The endoscopic approach allows for a minimally invasive and less painful alternative to traditional open surgery.

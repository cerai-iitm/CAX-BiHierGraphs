Occlusion of Ductus Arteriosus with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the placement of an intraluminal device through a small incision in the groin, guided by fluoroscopy and echocardiography, to occlude the ductus arteriosus, a blood vessel that connects the aorta and pulmonary artery. The device is advanced through a catheter to the ductus arteriosus, where it is deployed to block the flow of blood, allowing the heart to function more efficiently. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and carries a low risk of complications. The device remains in place permanently, and the patient usually requires a follow-up echocardiogram to ensure the defect is closed.

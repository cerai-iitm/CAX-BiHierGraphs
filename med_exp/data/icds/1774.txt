Excision of Left Inguinal Lymphatic, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
During a diagnostic percutaneous excision procedure, a small incision is made in the skin to access the left inguinal lymph node, which is then removed through a minimally invasive approach. This procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia to numb the area, allowing the patient to remain awake during the procedure. The removed lymph node is then sent to a laboratory for examination to determine if cancer has spread to the lymph node. The percutaneous approach minimizes tissue trauma, reduces scarring, and promotes faster healing and recovery.

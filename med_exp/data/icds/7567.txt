Transfusion of Nonautologous Globulin into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This medical procedure involves the administration of nonautologous globulin, a type of protein, into a peripheral vein through a percutaneous approach. The globulin is derived from a donor's blood or plasma and is used to treat various medical conditions, such as immunodeficiency disorders or bleeding disorders. The percutaneous approach involves making a small incision in the skin to access the vein, and the globulin is then injected into the vein through a needle or catheter. The goal of this transfusion is to provide the necessary proteins to the patient's bloodstream, which can help to prevent bleeding, infection, or other complications.

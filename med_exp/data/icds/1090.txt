Dilation of Left Femoral Artery, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves making a small incision in the skin to access the left femoral artery, allowing for the insertion of a balloon-tipped catheter to widen the narrowed or blocked portion of the artery. The catheter is guided through the artery using fluoroscopic imaging, and the balloon is inflated to compress plaque and dilate the artery, improving blood flow to the leg. After the procedure, the catheter and balloon are removed, and the incision site is closed. This treatment helps to alleviate symptoms of peripheral artery disease, such as leg pain or weakness, and reduces the risk of complications.

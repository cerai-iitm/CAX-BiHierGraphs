Drainage of Pericardial Cavity, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a thin tube or catheter through the skin and into the pericardial sac, a fluid-filled space surrounding the heart, to drain excess fluid that may be compressing the heart and impeding its function. Guided by imaging techniques such as echocardiography or fluoroscopy, the healthcare provider advances the catheter into the pericardial cavity to aspirate the accumulated fluid, which may be sent to a laboratory for diagnostic analysis. The procedure is typically performed in a hospital setting under local anesthesia and may take around 30-60 minutes to complete. Following the procedure, the catheter is removed, and the puncture site is closed, allowing the patient to recover and return to normal activities.

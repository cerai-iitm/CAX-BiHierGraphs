Supplement Lumbar Vertebral Joint with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves opening the skin and soft tissues to access the lumbar vertebral joint, with the goal of providing additional support and stability to the joint. A nonautologous tissue substitute, such as a bone graft or synthetic material, is then inserted into the joint to supplement and reinforce it. This open approach allows the surgeon to directly visualize the joint and surrounding tissues, ensuring precise placement of the tissue substitute. The procedure is typically performed to treat conditions such as spinal instability, degenerative disc disease, or herniated discs, with the aim of reducing pain and improving mobility.

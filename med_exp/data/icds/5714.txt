Fusion of 8 or more Thoracic Vertebral Joints with Autologous Tissue Substitute, Posterior Approach, Posterior Column, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves the fusion of eight or more thoracic vertebral joints using an autologous tissue substitute, which is a type of biological material harvested from the patient's own body. The procedure is performed through a posterior approach, where the surgeon accesses the spine from the back, and involves an open incision to expose the posterior column of the spine. The goal of the procedure is to stabilize the thoracic spine and alleviate symptoms such as pain, numbness, or weakness caused by conditions like spinal degeneration, fractures, or deformities. During the procedure, the autologous tissue substitute is carefully placed between the affected vertebrae to facilitate bone growth and fusion, ultimately promoting spinal stability and alignment.

Drainage of Right Upper Lobe Bronchus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
During a bronchoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is inserted through the mouth or nose to visualize the airways. The healthcare provider uses this scope to guide the drainage of accumulated fluids, mucus, or other debris from the right upper lobe bronchus, which is a section of the bronchial tube that branches off the trachea. This minimally invasive procedure is performed to diagnose and relieve respiratory symptoms such as coughing, wheezing, or shortness of breath. The goal is to restore normal airflow and promote healing in the affected lung tissue.

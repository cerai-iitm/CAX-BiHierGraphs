Excision of Large Intestine, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This is a minimally invasive procedure where a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, called an endoscope, is inserted through a natural or artificial opening to visualize the large intestine. The objective is to diagnose potential issues or abnormalities in the large intestine, such as polyps, ulcers, or tumors, which can then be removed through the same endoscope. The procedure allows for real-time visualization of the internal structures, enabling the healthcare provider to identify and excise any problematic tissue. It is typically performed under conscious sedation, and patients usually experience minimal discomfort and quick recovery.

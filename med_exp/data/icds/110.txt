Extirpation of Matter from Intracranial Epidural Space, Open Approach
Surgical removal of abnormal material or substances from the intracranial epidural space, the area between the skull and the outer lining of the brain, is performed through an open approach, involving a craniotomy to access the affected area. This procedure is typically done to treat conditions such as epidural hematomas, abscesses, or tumors that require emergency intervention. The surgeon makes an incision in the scalp, removes a section of skull bone, and carefully extracts the unwanted material from the epidural space to alleviate pressure on the brain. After the procedure, the incision is closed, and the patient is closely monitored for postoperative complications.

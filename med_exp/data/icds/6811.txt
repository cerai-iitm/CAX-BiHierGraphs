Control Bleeding in Chest Wall, Open Approach
During an open chest surgery, a healthcare provider manually stops bleeding in the chest wall by visually identifying and addressing the source of hemorrhage, utilizing sutures, ligation, or cautery as necessary to achieve hemostasis. This procedure is often performed in emergency situations, such as following trauma or during cardiothoracic surgery, to prevent life-threatening complications. The surgeon makes a deliberate incision in the chest wall to directly visualize and control the bleeding, which may involve repairing or removing damaged blood vessels, ligating bleeding arteries, or applying pressure to the affected area. By directly accessing the bleeding site, the surgeon can effectively manage bleeding and promote recovery.

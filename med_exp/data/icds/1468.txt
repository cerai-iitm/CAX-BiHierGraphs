Insertion of Infusion Device into Right External Jugular Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves inserting a small medical device, such as a port or pump, under the skin and attaching it to a catheter that is placed into the right external jugular vein. The insertion is done percutaneously, meaning through the skin, rather than through an open incision. The device is used to deliver medications, fluids, or nutrients directly into the bloodstream, and can remain in place for an extended period of time. The right external jugular vein is a superficial vein located in the neck, and is a commonly used site for central venous access.

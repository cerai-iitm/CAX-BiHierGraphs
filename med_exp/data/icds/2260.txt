Extirpation of Matter from Carina, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This procedure involves the use of an endoscope to visualize the carina, a keel-like structure located at the bifurcation of the trachea, and remove any unwanted matter or debris that may be obstructing the airway. The endoscope is inserted through a natural or artificial opening, allowing the healthcare provider to directly view the carina and surrounding tissues. Using specialized instruments, the provider then carefully extracts the unwanted material, which can help to relieve symptoms such as difficulty breathing, coughing, or wheezing. The entire process is typically performed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia to ensure the patient's comfort and safety.

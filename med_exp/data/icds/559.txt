Replacement of Aortic Valve with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves replacing the aortic valve with a non-autologous tissue substitute, which is a valve made from animal tissue or synthetic materials, using an open approach, where the chest is opened to access the heart. The diseased or damaged aortic valve is removed and the new valve is sewn into place, restoring normal blood flow and pressure. The procedure is usually performed under general anesthesia and may require the use of a heart-lung machine to maintain circulation during the operation. The goal of the surgery is to improve cardiac function, alleviate symptoms, and enhance overall quality of life.

Excision of Right Diaphragm, Open Approach, Diagnostic
This surgical procedure involves making an incision in the chest or abdominal cavity to access the right diaphragm, a dome-shaped muscle that separates the chest cavity from the abdominal cavity. The goal of this open approach is to excise or remove a portion of the right diaphragm for diagnostic purposes, which may be necessary to diagnose conditions such as diaphragmatic hernias, tumors, or other abnormalities. During the procedure, the surgeon will visually examine the diaphragm and surrounding tissues to identify any abnormalities and collect tissue samples for further examination. The procedure typically requires general anesthesia and may involve a hospital stay for post-operative care and monitoring.

Insertion of Short-term External Heart Assist System into Heart, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the insertion of a short-term external heart assist system into the heart through a percutaneous approach, providing temporary circulatory support to patients in cardiogenic shock or with heart failure. The system is inserted through a small incision in the groin area, and its cannulae are advanced through the femoral artery and vein to the heart. Once in place, the system takes over the pumping function of the heart, increasing cardiac output and improving blood flow to vital organs. The procedure is typically performed in an emergency setting, such as a cardiac catheterization laboratory or an operating room, under local anesthesia and fluoroscopic guidance.

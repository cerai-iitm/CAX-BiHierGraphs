Introduction of Other Therapeutic Substance into Pleural Cavity, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
A medical professional uses a minimally invasive percutaneous endoscopic approach to introduce a therapeutic substance into the pleural cavity, which is the space between the lungs and chest wall. This procedure is typically performed to treat conditions such as malignant pleural effusion, a buildup of fluid in the pleural cavity caused by cancer. The therapeutic substance, often a sclerosing agent or chemotherapeutic agent, is injected through the skin and guided into the pleural cavity using an endoscope, a thin flexible tube with a camera and light on the end. The goal of the procedure is to promote healing, reduce fluid buildup, and alleviate symptoms such as chest pain and difficulty breathing.

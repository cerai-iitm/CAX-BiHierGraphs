Insertion of Feeding Device into Jejunum, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This procedure involves the insertion of a feeding device into the jejunum, a part of the small intestine, using a percutaneous endoscopic approach. A flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, called an endoscope, is inserted through the mouth and guided through the stomach and into the small intestine. The feeding device is then placed through the skin and into the jejunum, allowing for nutrition to be directly administered into the small intestine. This procedure is typically performed under sedation and with the guidance of fluoroscopy or ultrasound to ensure proper placement of the feeding device.

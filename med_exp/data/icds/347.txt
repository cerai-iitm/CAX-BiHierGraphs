Dilation of Coronary Artery, One Artery with Two Drug-eluting Intraluminal Devices, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves widening a narrowed or blocked coronary artery, a major blood vessel supplying the heart, using a minimally invasive percutaneous approach. A specialized balloon-tipped catheter is inserted through a small incision in the arm or leg and guided to the blockage, where it is inflated to compress the plaque and restore blood flow. Two drug-eluting stents are then deployed to prop open the artery and prevent further blockage, releasing medication to inhibit tissue growth and promote healing. This precise and controlled procedure can help alleviate symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain or shortness of breath.

Extirpation of Matter from Right Popliteal Artery, Open Approach
During this surgical procedure, an incision is made in the popliteal fossa, a diamond-shaped region at the back of the knee, to access the right popliteal artery. The artery is then opened, and any unwanted matter, such as plaque, blood clots, or other debris, is removed to restore blood flow and prevent further complications. The procedure is typically performed under general or regional anesthesia to minimize discomfort and ensure a successful outcome. After the extirpation, the artery is closed, and the incision site is sutured or stapled to promote healing and reduce the risk of infection.

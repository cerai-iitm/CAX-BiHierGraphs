Repair Ventricular Septum, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves repairing a hole or defect in the ventricular septum, the wall of tissue that separates the lower chambers of the heart, through a small puncture in the skin. Using advanced imaging guidance, a catheter is inserted through the puncture site and guided to the heart, allowing the physician to visualize the defect. Once in place, a device is deployed through the catheter to close the hole, restoring normal blood flow and preventing further complications. The percutaneous approach eliminates the need for open-heart surgery, reducing recovery time and potentially minimizing scarring.

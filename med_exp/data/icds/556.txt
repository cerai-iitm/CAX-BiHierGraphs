Replacement of Chordae Tendineae with Synthetic Substitute, Open Approach
This procedure involves the surgical replacement of damaged or diseased chordae tendineae, which are thin fibrous cords in the heart that connect the papillary muscles to the tricuspid or mitral valve leaflets. During the open approach, the surgeon makes a large incision in the chest to access the heart and replaces the defective chordae tendineae with synthetic substitutes, which are designed to mimic the natural structure and function of the original cords. The synthetic substitutes are typically made of durable materials such as polyester or polypropylene and are designed to withstand the constant stress and strain of the heart's pumping action. The goal of the procedure is to restore normal valve function and alleviate symptoms such as shortness of breath, fatigue, and swelling.

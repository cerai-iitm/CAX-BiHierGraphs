Introduction of Other Diagnostic Substance into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
A percutaneous approach is used to introduce a diagnostic substance directly into the central vein, allowing healthcare professionals to gather vital diagnostic information. This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a small needle or catheter through the skin and into the central vein, typically in the arm or chest area. The diagnostic substance, such as a dye or contrast agent, is then injected through the catheter and tracked as it flows through the bloodstream, providing real-time images and data for diagnostic analysis. This procedure is often used to diagnose and monitor a range of conditions, including vascular diseases, blood clots, and organ dysfunction.

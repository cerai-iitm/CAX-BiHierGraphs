Fluoroscopy of Pancreatic Ducts using Other Contrast
This procedure involves the use of fluoroscopy, a type of real-time X-ray imaging, to visualize the pancreatic ducts, which are narrow tubes that carry digestive enzymes from the pancreas to the small intestine. During the procedure, a contrast agent is injected into the pancreatic ducts to enhance visibility, and a fluoroscope is used to guide the procedure and capture images of the ducts. The use of other contrast agents, such as iohexol or gadolinium, helps to highlight the pancreatic ducts and surrounding tissue, allowing for a more detailed evaluation of the area. The resulting images aid in the diagnosis and treatment of pancreatic disorders, such as pancreatic duct stones, strictures, or tumors.

Supplement Radial Nerve with Nonautologous Tissue Substitute, Open Approach
During this surgical procedure, a non-autologous tissue substitute is used to supplement the radial nerve, which is responsible for controlling various motor and sensory functions in the arm and hand. The open approach involves making an incision in the skin and underlying tissues to access the radial nerve, allowing the surgeon to visually inspect the nerve and implant the tissue substitute. This may be necessary to repair or replace damaged or diseased nerve tissue, restoring normal function to the radial nerve and alleviating symptoms such as numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arm and hand. The use of a non-autologous tissue substitute can provide a biocompatible and durable solution for nerve repair, promoting optimal recovery and rehabilitation.

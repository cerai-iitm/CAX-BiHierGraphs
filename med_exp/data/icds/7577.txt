Transfusion of Nonautologous Globulin into Central Vein, Percutaneous Approach
This medical procedure involves the administration of nonautologous globulin, a type of protein, directly into a central vein through a percutaneous approach, which means the insertion of a needle or catheter through the skin to access the vein. The globulin is obtained from a donor and is used to treat various conditions, such as immunodeficiency disorders or bleeding disorders. The procedure is typically performed in a hospital setting under the supervision of a healthcare professional, and the patient is closely monitored for any adverse reactions. The goal of the procedure is to provide the necessary globulin to help the patient's body function properly and alleviate symptoms.

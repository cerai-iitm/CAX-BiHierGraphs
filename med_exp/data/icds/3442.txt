Extirpation of Matter from Common Hepatic Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of unwanted matter from the common hepatic duct, a vital structure that carries bile from the liver and gallbladder to the small intestine. Through a natural or artificial opening, a flexible tube with a camera and specialized instruments is inserted to visualize the duct and extract the obstructing material. The goal of the procedure is to restore proper bile flow and alleviate symptoms such as jaundice, abdominal pain, and fever. The minimally invasive nature of the procedure reduces the risk of complications and promotes a faster recovery compared to traditional open surgery.

Dilation of Coronary Artery, Three Arteries with Drug-eluting Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves inserting a catheter through an artery in the leg or arm to reach the coronary arteries, which supply blood to the heart. Using X-ray guidance, a doctor inflates a balloon to widen the narrowed or blocked sections of three coronary arteries, restoring blood flow to the heart. A drug-eluting stent, a tiny mesh tube coated with medication, is then placed in each artery to keep them open and prevent future blockages. The stent slowly releases medication over time to prevent scarring and promote healing, reducing the risk of future cardiac events.

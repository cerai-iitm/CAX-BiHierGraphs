Bypass Coronary Artery, Three Arteries from Coronary Artery, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves creating a detour around a blocked coronary artery by connecting a healthy blood vessel to three coronary arteries, restoring blood flow to the heart. The open approach requires a thoracotomy, where the chest is opened to access the heart and coronary arteries. The surgeon then grafts a healthy blood vessel, typically taken from the patient's leg or arm, to the blocked coronary arteries, bypassing the blockage and restoring blood flow. The goal of this complex procedure is to relieve symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain and shortness of breath, and to reduce the risk of heart attack.

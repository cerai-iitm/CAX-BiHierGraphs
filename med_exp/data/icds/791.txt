Insertion of Intraluminal Device into Right Subclavian Artery, Percutaneous Approach
During this minimally invasive procedure, a small incision is made in the skin above the right subclavian artery, and a thin tube called a catheter is inserted through the incision. The catheter is guided through the artery using fluoroscopic imaging until it reaches the desired location, where an intraluminal device is then inserted to maintain patency and improve blood flow. The device is designed to reduce the risk of artery narrowing or blockage, and the percutaneous approach allows for less tissue damage and trauma compared to open surgery. Following the procedure, the catheter is removed, and the incision site is closed with stitches or a small bandage.

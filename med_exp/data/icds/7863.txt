Extracorporeal Oxygenation, Membrane, Peripheral Veno-venous
This therapeutic procedure involves the use of a membrane oxygenator to provide extracorporeal oxygenation to the patient's blood. The peripheral veno-venous approach is utilized, where a cannula is inserted into a peripheral vein, typically in the leg, and advanced to the vena cava to extract deoxygenated blood. The blood is then pumped through the oxygenator, which adds oxygen and removes carbon dioxide, before being returned to the patient through a separate cannula. This artificial oxygenation process supports the patient's cardiopulmonary function and can be used in various clinical scenarios, such as cardiac surgery, respiratory failure, or cardiogenic shock.

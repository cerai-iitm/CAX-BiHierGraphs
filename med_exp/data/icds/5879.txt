Drainage of Left Metatarsal-Phalangeal Joint, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous drainage of the left metatarsal-phalangeal joint is a minimally invasive procedure used to remove excess fluid or pus from the joint space. The procedure involves inserting a small needle or drainage catheter through the skin and into the joint, guided by imaging studies such as X-ray or ultrasonography. The drain is left in place for a certain period to allow for continuous drainage, and is usually removed once the fluid accumulation has resolved. This procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as joint infections, gout, or rheumatoid arthritis that cause painful swelling and inflammation in the joint.

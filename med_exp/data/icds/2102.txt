Drainage of Left Main Bronchus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end through the mouth or nose to reach the left main bronchus, allowing visualization of the airway and insertion of a drainage device to remove mucus, pus, or other debris. The procedure is typically performed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia to ensure patient comfort and cooperation. The endoscope is guided through the natural opening of the mouth or nose, or through an artificial opening created by a bronchoscopy, to access the left main bronchus. The drainage device is then inserted through the endoscope to suction out any blockages or secretions, helping to restore airflow and relieve respiratory distress.

Insertion of Feeding Device into Duodenum, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves the insertion of a feeding device, typically a percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) tube, through the skin and into the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine. The procedure is usually performed under endoscopic guidance, where a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is inserted through the mouth and stomach to guide the placement of the feeding tube. The feeding tube is then advanced through the abdominal wall and into the duodenum, allowing for nutrition to be delivered directly into the small intestine. This procedure is often used for patients who have difficulty eating or swallowing, or require long-term nutritional support.

Occlusion of Stomach with Intraluminal Device, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope to guide an intraluminal device through the mouth and esophagus into the stomach, where it is deployed to occlude the stomach. The device is designed to block the stomach, preventing food and liquids from entering, and is typically used to treat conditions such as gastrointestinal bleeding or obstruction. The percutaneous endoscopic approach allows for precise placement of the device under direct visualization, minimizing the risk of complications. The procedure is usually performed under conscious sedation, and patients are typically able to recover quickly with minimal discomfort.

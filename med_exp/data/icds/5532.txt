Supplement Lumbar Vertebra with Synthetic Substitute, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves making a small incision in the skin to access the lumbar vertebra, after which a synthetic substitute, such as a bone graft or artificial disc, is inserted to replace or augment the damaged or diseased vertebral body. The percutaneous approach allows for reduced tissue trauma and scarring compared to open surgery, and is typically performed under fluoroscopic or CT guidance to ensure accurate placement of the substitute. The goal of this procedure is to restore spinal stability, alleviate pain, and improve mobility by providing structural support to the affected lumbar vertebra. By using a synthetic substitute, the natural spine anatomy can be preserved, and the risk of complications associated with harvesting bone grafts from another part of the body is avoided.

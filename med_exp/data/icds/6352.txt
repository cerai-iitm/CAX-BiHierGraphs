Excision of Bladder, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This minimally invasive surgical procedure involves the removal of the bladder through a natural or artificial opening using an endoscope, a thin, flexible tube with a camera and light on the end. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and allows surgeons to visualize the bladder and surrounding tissues in real-time, enabling precise excision of the bladder. The endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications and promotes faster recovery compared to traditional open surgery. The procedure is usually performed to treat conditions such as bladder cancer, benign tumors, or other diseases that necessitate the removal of the bladder.

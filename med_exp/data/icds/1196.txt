Extirpation of Matter from Right Peroneal Artery, Percutaneous Approach
During this minimally invasive procedure, a catheter is inserted through the skin and guided to the right peroneal artery to remove unwanted matter, such as blood clots or plaque, that may be blocking blood flow to the lower leg. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and imaging guidance, such as fluoroscopy or ultrasound, to ensure precise navigation and removal of the obstruction. The goal of this treatment is to restore blood flow to the affected area, alleviating symptoms such as pain, numbness, or weakness in the lower leg. Following the procedure, the catheter is removed, and the small incision site is closed, often with a small bandage.

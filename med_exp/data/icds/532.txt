Removal of Infusion Device from Great Vessel, External Approach
This procedure involves the surgical removal of an infusion device, such as a central line or implantable port, that has been placed in a great vessel, which includes major blood vessels like the aorta or vena cava. The external approach means the surgeon makes an incision in the skin to access the vessel and remove the device. The procedure typically requires general anesthesia and is performed in an operating room under sterile conditions. The removal of the infusion device is often necessary when it is no longer needed, is causing complications, or is malfunctioning, and is usually followed by closure of the incision and monitoring for any post-operative complications.

Drainage of Right Shoulder Joint, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the removal of fluid or other substances from the right shoulder joint through a small incision in the skin, using a needle or catheter inserted through the skin and into the joint space. The goal of this diagnostic procedure is to collect a sample of fluid or tissue for further examination, allowing for a more accurate diagnosis of the underlying condition causing symptoms such as pain, swelling, or limited mobility in the shoulder. The percutaneous approach ensures minimal tissue disruption and scarring, and the procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia to minimize discomfort. Once the sample is obtained, it is sent to a laboratory for analysis, which can help identify conditions such as infection, inflammation, or arthritis.

Removal of Neurostimulator Lead from Brain, Open Approach
This procedure involves surgically exposing the brain to directly visualize the neurostimulator lead, which is then carefully grasped and gently pulled out to prevent damage to surrounding brain tissue. The surgical site is then closed, usually with sutures or staples, to promote healing and minimize risk of infection. The removal of the neurostimulator lead is typically performed under general anesthesia to ensure the patient's comfort and immobility during the procedure. The goal of this procedure is to safely and effectively remove the lead, which may have been implanted to treat conditions such as Parkinson's disease, chronic pain, or epilepsy, and is no longer functioning or is causing complications.

Excision of Cecum, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of the cecum, a part of the large intestine, through a natural or artificial opening. The procedure is typically performed using an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, which allows the surgeon to visualize the area and precisely remove the affected tissue. The approach may be through the anus or a small incision in the abdomen, depending on the patient's condition and the surgeon's preference. The goal of the procedure is to treat conditions such as colon cancer, diverticulitis, or inflammatory bowel disease, and to alleviate symptoms such as abdominal pain, bleeding, or bowel obstruction.

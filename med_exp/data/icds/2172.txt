Excision of Carina, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic, Diagnostic
This endoscopic procedure involves the removal of the carina, a landmark structure located at the bifurcation of the trachea, through a natural or artificial opening. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and utilizes an endoscope to visualize the carina and guide the excision. The carina is carefully dissected and removed, and the surrounding tissue is inspected for any signs of disease or abnormality. The procedure is usually performed for diagnostic purposes, such as to obtain a tissue sample for biopsy or to identify any abnormalities in the tracheal bifurcation.

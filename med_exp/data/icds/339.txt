Destruction of Ventricular Septum, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves using a catheter to reach the ventricular septum through a small incision in the leg or arm. Once in place, a specialized device is deployed to create a small hole in the septum, allowing oxygen-rich blood to flow from the left ventricle into the right ventricle, improving cardiac function. The goal of this procedure is to reduce symptoms of heart failure, such as shortness of breath and fatigue, by increasing oxygen delivery to the body. Under fluoroscopic guidance, the interventional cardiologist carefully navigates the catheter to the precise location, ensuring accurate placement of the device to achieve optimal results.

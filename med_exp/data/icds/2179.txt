Excision of Right Upper Lobe Bronchus, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This percutaneous endoscopic procedure involves the removal of the right upper lobe bronchus through a minimally invasive approach, utilizing an endoscope inserted through the skin to visualize and dissect the bronchial tissue. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and requires precise navigation through the bronchial tree to identify and excise the targeted lobe. The percutaneous endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications and post-operative pain compared to traditional open surgical methods. The excised tissue is then examined for diagnostic purposes, such as to identify cancer or other disease processes affecting the lung tissue.

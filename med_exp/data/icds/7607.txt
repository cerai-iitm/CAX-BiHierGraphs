Introduction of Other Thrombolytic into Peripheral Vein, Percutaneous Approach
Percutaneous introduction of a thrombolytic agent into a peripheral vein is a minimally invasive procedure used to dissolve blood clots and restore blood flow. Through a small incision in the skin, a catheter is inserted into the affected vein, and the thrombolytic medication is infused directly into the clot. This procedure is typically performed in a hospital setting under the guidance of imaging technology, such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy. The goal of this treatment is to rapidly restore blood flow and prevent further tissue damage or complications.

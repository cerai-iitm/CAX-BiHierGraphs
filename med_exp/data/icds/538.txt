Repair Atrial Septum, Percutaneous Approach
This minimally invasive procedure involves using a catheter to repair a hole in the wall separating the upper chambers of the heart, which is a congenital defect that can cause oxygenated blood to flow back into the lungs instead of the rest of the body. Through a small incision in the leg, a flexible tube with a tiny umbrella-like device is guided to the heart, where the device is opened to block the hole. Once in place, the device is released, and the catheter is removed, allowing the heart to function normally. The procedure is typically done under general anesthesia and may require a short hospital stay for observation.

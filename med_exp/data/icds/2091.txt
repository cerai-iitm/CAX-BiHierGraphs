Drainage of Carina, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
During an endoscopic procedure, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end is inserted through the mouth or nose to reach the carina, a region where the trachea divides into two bronchi. The scope allows visualization of the area and identification of any blockages or abnormalities. The healthcare provider then uses specialized instruments to drain any accumulated fluid, mucus, or debris from the carina, restoring normal respiratory function. This minimally invasive procedure is often performed under conscious sedation, and patients typically experience significant improvement in breathing and coughing symptoms afterwards.

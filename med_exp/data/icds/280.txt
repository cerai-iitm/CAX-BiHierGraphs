Bypass Coronary Artery, One Artery from Coronary Artery with Autologous Venous Tissue, Open Approach
A surgical procedure is performed to bypass a blocked coronary artery by creating a new pathway for blood to flow to the heart using a piece of vein taken from the patient's own body. The procedure involves making an incision in the chest to access the heart and coronary arteries, and then grafting the autologous venous tissue onto the blocked coronary artery to restore blood flow. The goal of the procedure is to relieve symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain and shortness of breath, and to reduce the risk of heart attack. The open approach allows the surgeon to visually inspect the coronary arteries and perform the bypass grafting with precision.

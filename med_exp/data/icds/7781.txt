Measurement of Cardiac Output, Percutaneous Approach
This non-invasive medical procedure involves using a percutaneous approach to measure a patient's cardiac output, which is the volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute. The technique is typically used to assess cardiovascular function, particularly in critically ill patients or those undergoing surgical procedures. During the procedure, a specialized probe or sensor is inserted through the skin to measure blood flow and pressure, allowing healthcare professionals to calculate cardiac output accurately. The data obtained helps clinicians to diagnose and monitor conditions such as heart failure, sepsis, and circulatory shock.

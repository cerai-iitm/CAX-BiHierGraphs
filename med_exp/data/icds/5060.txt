Reposition Cervical Vertebra with Internal Fixation Device, Open Approach
This surgical procedure involves repositioning one or multiple cervical vertebrae to their normal anatomical alignment through an open approach, where a large incision is made to access the spine. Internal fixation devices, such as plates, screws, or rods, are then used to stabilize and hold the vertebrae in place, promoting healing and fusion. The goal of the procedure is to relieve symptoms caused by spinal instability, deformity, or malalignment, such as pain, numbness, or weakness in the neck, arms, or legs. The procedure requires careful planning and execution, as it involves manipulating delicate spinal structures to achieve optimal spinal alignment and stability.

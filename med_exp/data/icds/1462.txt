Insertion of Infusion Device into Right Basilic Vein, Percutaneous Approach
The percutaneous insertion of an infusion device into the right basilic vein is a minimally invasive procedure that allows for the administration of medications, nutrients, or other fluids directly into the bloodstream. During the procedure, a small incision is made in the skin and a catheter is guided through the basilic vein, a superficial vein located in the upper arm, using imaging guidance such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy. The infusion device is then secured in place and connected to a pump or syringe, enabling precise control over the rate and volume of fluid administration. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and can be completed in a matter of minutes.

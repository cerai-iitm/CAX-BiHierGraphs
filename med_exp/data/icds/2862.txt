Excision of Cecum, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This procedure involves the removal of the cecum, a pouch-like structure at the beginning of the large intestine, through a minimally invasive approach using an endoscope inserted through the skin. The endoscope provides visualization of the internal organs, allowing the surgeon to carefully excise the cecum while minimizing damage to surrounding tissues. This percutaneous procedure eliminates the need for open surgery, resulting in reduced scarring, less post-operative pain, and faster recovery times. The excision of the cecum is typically performed to treat conditions such as cancer, diverticulitis, or inflammation of the cecum.

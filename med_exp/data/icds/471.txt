Insertion of Intracardiac Pacemaker into Right Ventricle, Percutaneous Approach
This procedure involves placing a pacemaker directly into the right ventricle of the heart through a small incision in the skin, using a percutaneous approach. The pacemaker is inserted through a thin tube called a catheter, which is guided through a large vein in the groin area and into the heart. Once in place, the pacemaker helps regulate the heart's rhythm, ensuring a consistent and healthy heartbeat. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and takes around 30-60 minutes to complete.

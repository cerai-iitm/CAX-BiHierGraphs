Respiratory Ventilation, Greater than 96 Consecutive Hours
This medical procedure involves the use of mechanical ventilation to support a patient's breathing for an extended period, exceeding 96 hours continuously. During this time, a machine takes over the work of breathing, supplying oxygen and removing carbon dioxide from the lungs, allowing the patient's respiratory muscles to rest. The ventilator is set up to deliver a specific volume and pressure of air to the patient's lungs, with adjustments made as needed to ensure optimal oxygenation and ventilation. Close monitoring by healthcare providers is crucial to detect any changes in the patient's condition and make necessary adjustments to the ventilator settings.

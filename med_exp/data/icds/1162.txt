Extirpation of Matter from Right Common Iliac Artery, Open Approach
This procedure involves making an incision in the abdominal wall to access the right common iliac artery, which is a major blood vessel that supplies oxygenated blood to the lower limbs. The surgeon then carefully opens the artery to remove unwanted matter, such as blood clots, plaque, or other debris, that may be obstructing blood flow or causing damage to the vessel. The goal of this procedure is to restore normal blood flow and prevent potential complications, such as limb ischemia or gangrene. The surgery typically requires general anesthesia and is often performed in a hospital setting by a vascular surgeon or a cardiothoracic surgeon.

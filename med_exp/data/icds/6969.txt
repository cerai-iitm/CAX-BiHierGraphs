Excision of Retroperitoneum, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach, Diagnostic
This minimally invasive procedure involves the use of an endoscope, a flexible tube with a camera and light on the end, to visualize the retroperitoneum, a space in the back of the abdomen. The endoscope is inserted through a small incision in the skin, allowing the surgeon to examine the area and identify any abnormalities or lesions. Tissue samples may be removed for biopsy or other diagnostic purposes. The procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia and sedation, and recovery time is generally shorter compared to open surgical approaches.

Drainage of Common Bile Duct, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
Endoscopic drainage of the common bile duct is a minimally invasive procedure that involves the insertion of a specialized endoscope through the mouth or a small incision to access the bile duct. Through the endoscope, a catheter or drainage tube is guided into the bile duct to remove blockages, stones, or other obstructions, allowing for the free flow of bile. This procedure is often performed to treat conditions such as choledocholithiasis, where stones are present in the bile duct, or cholangitis, an infection of the bile duct. The endoscopic approach reduces the risk of complications and allows for a quicker recovery compared to open surgical procedures.

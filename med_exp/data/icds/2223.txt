Excision of Left Upper Lung Lobe, Percutaneous Endoscopic Approach
This procedure involves the removal of the left upper lung lobe through a minimally invasive percutaneous endoscopic approach, which requires making small incisions in the chest wall. The use of an endoscope, a thin flexible tube with a camera and light, allows the surgeon to visualize the lung tissue and precisely identify the area to be excised. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may be necessary to treat conditions such as lung cancer, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), or benign tumorous growths. The percutaneous endoscopic approach offers several benefits, including reduced scarring, less post-operative pain, and a shorter hospital stay.

Destruction of Esophagus, Via Natural or Artificial Opening Endoscopic
This endoscopic procedure involves the insertion of a flexible tube with a camera and light through the mouth or a surgically created opening to reach the esophagus. Under visual guidance, the surgeon uses specialized instruments to destroy or remove esophageal tissue or lesions, which may be cancerous or benign. The procedure is typically performed under general anesthesia and may require several hours to complete. After the procedure, the patient may experience discomfort, bleeding, or difficulty swallowing, and may need to stay in the hospital for a few days for monitoring and recovery.

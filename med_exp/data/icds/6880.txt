Drainage of Left Pleural Cavity, Percutaneous Approach, Diagnostic
This procedure involves the insertion of a drainage tube through the skin and into the left pleural cavity to remove excess fluid or air that has accumulated in the space between the lungs and chest wall. The percutaneous approach means the procedure is done through the skin, rather than through a large incision, and is typically performed under imaging guidance, such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy. The goal of the procedure is to diagnose and treat conditions such as pleural effusion, pneumothorax, or hemothorax, which can cause symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, and coughing. The procedure allows for the removal of fluid or air, relieving pressure on the lungs and helping to restore normal breathing function.
